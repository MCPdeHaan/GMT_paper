# Research brief and draft outreach

Prepared 5 September 2026. These are drafts for Maurits to adapt and send. Nobody has been contacted. The proposed research direction is provisional and makes no claim that revised results already exist.

## A concise research brief for an initial meeting

**Working title:** What can public data tell us about Global Minimum Tax revenue? Measurement, legal allocation and the reconciliation of European estimates.

**Research question.** Which assumptions account for differences in public-data estimates of European GMT top-up revenue, and which conclusions survive alternative measurements and legal scenarios?

**Starting point.** My MSc Economic Policy thesis at Utrecht built a bilateral OECD CbCR pipeline for 2017–2022, with profit corrections, competing ETR measurements, substance exclusions, behavioural calibration and QDMTT/IIR/UTPR allocation. Its factorial analysis suggests that measurement and legal allocation are especially important, but I want to revisit the design and validation before presenting it as a journal contribution.

**Potential contribution.** The revised article would place alternative estimates on a comparable footing, distinguish top-up taxes from the ordinary-CIT consequences of reduced profit shifting, and show how limited public information constrains country revenue estimates. It would use FY2023 as a newer annual base and, where feasible, validation information. It would not claim to identify realised GMT effects from pre-implementation CbCR data.

**Position relative to existing work.** Hugger and coauthors establish the importance of within-jurisdiction ETR heterogeneity; Boukal and coauthors decompose top-up and ordinary-CIT effects using administrative data for Slovakia; the OECD has released an updated assessment and early post-implementation research in July 2026. I want to establish whether a transparent public-data reconciliation and validation exercise provides a sufficiently distinct contribution.

**Issues I will resolve first.** The interpretation and profit weighting of distributional inputs; consistency of nominal monetary units; the distinction between behavioural assumptions and legal safe harbours; and the plausibility of large country-level estimates. I am prepared to narrow the question or change the main finding if that is what the evidence requires.

**Proposed first outputs.** A comparison of the nearest studies; a validated minimum version of the revenue model; an FY2023 data-vintage and coverage assessment; and a small set of central tables with a concise article introduction. ITPF or Fiscal Studies are possible targets, depending on the eventual contribution.

**Longer-term ambition.** I want to develop a coherent external doctorate at a Dutch university on international taxation, with both academic quality and policy/business relevance. I can commit approximately 8–12 hours per week and have some employer flexibility. My objective is to agree a feasible supervisory and institutional route by June 2027; I do not expect a completed doctorate or necessarily an accepted paper by then.

**Working style.** I value direct substantive criticism, agreed milestones, independence in organising my work and reliable supervision. I would like to test collaboration on a bounded part of the paper before committing to a long-term arrangement.

**Specific request.** Feedback on whether this is a promising and sufficiently distinct article; the two changes that would most improve it; and whether there might be a suitable collaboration or supervisory arrangement.

Attach a two-page version of this brief, a one-page comparison of the nearest studies when completed, and a link to the thesis. Do not attach every intermediate output.
