# R05a UTPR evidence and method

**Evaluation date: 1 January 2026. Evidence cutoff: 15 November 2026.** The question is whether a national UTPR applies to a fiscal year **beginning** on 1 January 2026. The law is reconstructed using material published by the later cutoff. A later source can verify an earlier legal or fiscal date. A pending bill with proposed retrospective effect cannot establish an enacted charge. The 2017–2023 dates in the empirical work identify the economic observations used to model that common policy. The results are counterfactual model allocations.

## Legal basis

- **EU obligations and domestic authority.** Directive 2022/2523 Articles 12–14 provide for a UTPR backstop, generally from fiscal years beginning on 31 December 2024 under Article 56(1). Domestic legislation determines whether and when a jurisdiction has a charging mechanism. An enacted statute, its formal commencement, and the fiscal years that it reaches can have different dates. The UTPR may be implemented through a top-up tax or a denial of deductions that gives an equivalent adjustment. Under Article 12(2), an amount that could not be collected through the adjustment in one fiscal year can carry forward. The Directive, national legislation, and the OECD Model Rules and guidance have distinct legal roles. [Directive 2022/2523, Articles 12–14 and 56](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022L2523) · [OECD Model Rules and commentary](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two.html)
- **Article 50.** The Commission confirms the elections of Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta and Slovakia. The election allows Member States with at most twelve in-scope ultimate parents to delay IIR and UTPR for six consecutive fiscal years beginning from 31 December 2023. Under Article 50(2) another implementing Member State can collect its Article 14 share in respect of constituent entities there where the group is headquartered in an electing Member State, for fiscal years beginning from 31 December 2023. This is an earlier source-side rule with a separate recipient-side requirement. The deferring jurisdiction has no UTPR collector authority under that provision. Slovakia separately imposes a domestic top-up under Act 507/2023. The other four rows record no separate domestic top-up established at the evaluation date. Partial transposition and reporting duties do not supply an IIR or UTPR charge. R3's assumption of IIR and domestic top-up for all EU27 members is a model counterfactual. [Commission Notice C/2023/1536](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2023/1536/oj/eng) · [Slovak Act 507/2023](https://static.slov-lex.sk/pdf/SK/ZZ/2023/507/ZZ_2023_507.pdf) · [Lithuanian Act XIV-2680](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/94778e0028a611efb121d2fe3a0eff27/) · [Malta Tax and Customs Administration guidance](https://mtca.gov.mt/docs/default-source/documents/personal-tax/legal-and-technical/guidelines/guidance-note_eu-global-minimum-level-of-taxation-for-mne-groups-and-large-scale-domestic-groups-regulations-1.pdf)
- **Allocation under Articles 12–14.** The UTPR amount concerns a group and fiscal year. Article 14 calculates the residual after qualified IIR allocation, including zeroing a constituent entity's amount where all the UPE's ownership interests are held through parents required to apply a qualified IIR. The allocation uses equal weights on each implementing country's share of the group's employees and tangible assets (net book value) across the jurisdictions with a qualified UTPR in force for that fiscal year. It includes rules for employees and assets of permanent establishments and excludes investment entities from the allocation factors. Where allocations in prior fiscal years have failed to produce the corresponding additional cash tax expense, the jurisdiction's percentage is zero and its activity is excluded. If every qualified UTPR jurisdiction has a zero percentage, Article 14(9) disapplies that exclusion. Zero group-level allocation may also reflect zero relevant footprint. National authority alone establishes none of these group-specific amounts. [Directive, Articles 12–14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022L2523) · [OECD Model Rules, Chapter 2](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two.html)
- **Exclusions and safe harbours.** Article 49 provides an initial international activity exclusion for the relevant top-up for up to five years. Its group-level tests include a footprint in no more than six jurisdictions and no more than €50m in tangible assets outside the reference jurisdiction. The OECD transitional UTPR safe harbour generally deems the UTPR amount for the UPE jurisdiction to be zero where its nominal corporate income tax rate is at least 20%, for qualifying transitional fiscal years. The May 2026 guidance bridges a gap for certain 52–53 week years. The 2026 UPE Safe Harbour operates for qualifying UPE jurisdictions for fiscal years commencing from 1 January 2026 and concerns UTPR on profits in that jurisdiction. The broader SbS Safe Harbour for an eligible group's UPE jurisdiction concerns foreign IIR and UTPR and leaves domestic top-up available. These mechanisms have different dates, scopes, elections and predicates. The shadow applies the original SbS switch and preserves the other source assumptions. Further work needs group-level data or explicit assumptions on eligibility, threshold and take-up, along with due consideration of the applicable legal implementation and excluded entities under Articles 2 and 3. [Directive, Articles 2–3 and 49](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022L2523) · [OECD 52–53-week guidance](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/administrative-guidance-on-application-of-transitional-utpr-safe-harbour-to-mne-groups.pdf) · [OECD Side-by-Side package](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/side-by-side-package.pdf)
- **Qualification is a separate fact.** The pinned May 2026 Central Record transcription contains qualified IIR, QDMTT and SbS records. The public Central Record is a positive record of the statuses it covers. The September 2026 Inclusive Framework announcement establishes a framework for full legislative review, which covers the GloBE rules and domestic top-ups and follows the transitional qualification mechanism. That framework supplies no country-specific UTPR determination for this register. The register records OECD UTPR qualification as `not_established` for all rows. Neither an IIR listing nor absence of a UTPR listing establishes a UTPR qualification outcome. The VERIFIED arm verifies national applicability. [OECD Central Record](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/central-record-of-legislation-with-transitional-qualified-status.html) · [OECD September 2026 release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/announcements/2026/09/global-minimum-tax-inclusive-framework-releases-a-package-to-strengthen-consistency-and-certainty-for-mnes-and-jurisdictions.html)

## Register and source review

The [CSV register](../../data/legal/utpr_eligibility_2026.csv) has one row per ISO3, rule and evaluation date. The 50 candidate rows are all EU27 and all 23 non-EU recipients with positive weight in either frozen weight file. Their union covers all 44 positive-weight legacy collectors. We also screened the main non-legacy parent jurisdictions, the OECD implementation material and the domestic rules of likely omissions. Twelve separate parent-context rows cover the largest R1 universal UTPR source pools. They are outside both collector sensitivity arms unless the scope is revised with evidence. A parent-context screen is a limited check on the statute or administrative material cited, with no claim to a global legal census or an independent validation of every IIR. [OECD implementing-jurisdiction material](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/announcements/2026/05/global-minimum-tax-release-of-a-common-understanding-of-implementing-jurisdictions-and-further-administrative-guidance-to-support-compliance.html)

| Field or status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `verified_applicable` | An operative national UTPR reaches the reference fiscal-year start. National rule availability is verified separately from its OECD qualification and from group-level eligibility. |
| `verified_not_applicable` | The reference year precedes the enacted rule's applicable fiscal start, the legislation awaits a separate commencement decision, or the authoritative record shows the charge has not been enacted. These situations can be distinguished by the enactment and date fields and the source note. |
| `deferred` | A verified Article 50 election. The separate `article50_source_rule` records collection in other implementing Member States under Article 50(2). |
| `not_established` | Authoritative UTPR evidence is insufficient to classify applicability. Guernsey is the candidate in this category. The published charging instrument could not be retrieved from the legal gazette. The provisional arm includes it because the frozen model does. Parent-context cases remain outside this arm. |
| `legally_ambiguous` | A positive conflict or material interpretative ambiguity prevents classification of an identified national rule. The schema accepts this separately from an evidence gap. No candidate was assigned this status. |
| `enactment_status` and dates | Enactment status refers to the UTPR charge or enabling statute. Enactment, commencement of the instrument or relevant provision, and first applicable ordinary fiscal-year start are separate. `first_applicable_fy_start` is the statutory boundary, or the first ordinary start for an explicit accounting-year transition. The earlier Article 50(2) case is recorded in the source notes. Blank dates reflect unavailable or inapplicable fields. |
| `evidence_category` | `national_law` records official gazette or statutory text. `official_guidance` identifies published government, revenue authority or ministry materials, including an authority's statement of timing. `eu_notice` records evidence of an Article 50 election. `bill` is proposed law, with its legislative progress recorded. `official_search` is a limited screen, distinct from proof of non-application. Commercial and academic trackers were cross-checks or locator aids. |
| `source_publication_date`, `source_retrieval_date` | The publication field records the verified date where identifiable, including the instrument's publication where the source displays a consolidation. Retrieval records the snapshot of the review. Publication can postdate the evaluation. |
| `oecd_utpr_qualification`, `domestic_topup_status` | Independent UTPR qualification evidence, with lack of evidence kept as `not_established`. The domestic top-up check is recorded principally for Article 50 electors. Other fields remain `not_reviewed` unless separately checked. |

Cases whose dates matter for this comparison include Japan, where the National Tax Agency specifies financial years beginning from 1 April 2026, and Indonesia, which starts UTPR on 1 January 2026. Hong Kong's IIR and domestic tax start in 2025 while the enabling UTPR awaits a separate date. Canada’s enacted Global Minimum Tax Act and the status of Bill C-31 distinguish enacted charges from a proposal for retrospective effect. The United Kingdom is an example of an enacted rule whose fiscal application expressly predates Royal Assent. ## Legal facts and the allocation proxy

The frozen [Stage 05 flags](../../code/05_stage3_behavioral.R#L1665) take IIR, QDMTT and SbS records from the [pinned input](../../data/raw/pillar2_qdmtt/oecd_central_record_2026_05.csv). Within that function, `utpr_start`, `iir_utpr_start` and the supposedly explicit start copy `iir_start`. Their eligibility indicators all copy the IIR indicator evaluated on 1 January 2026. Independent UTPR row counts are NA. The parent field routes IIR. The affiliate fields govern recipient status in [Stage 06](../../code/06_stage4_5_revenue_allocation_sbs.R#L99). The public data provide no independent legal support for the 44 “explicit” labels. The model also lacks the ownership chain needed to determine intermediate or partially owned parents' actual IIR obligations.

Stage 06 reconstructs one UTPR footprint per parent jurisdiction, economic year and collector. It takes 50:50 employee and asset shares where both totals are positive and uses the observed factor alone otherwise. The year fallback recomputes shares using all observed eligible collector activity where the parent has no supported route. It substitutes the global sample footprint for missing group footprint. Article 14 provides no comparable pooled fallback. Historical CbCR is aggregated across groups by parent and affiliate jurisdiction. Even when a footprint is positive and the law is independently verified, the data cannot identify actual group workforce and assets at the required grain, prior uncollected allocations, ultimate ownership chains or administrative payments. A zero EU27 share is a supported non-EU route. A pool with neither a valid parent-year route nor a valid year route remains unsupported.

The shadow calls the same [validated R04 route expansion](../../code/allocation/collector_routes.R) with rebuilt denominators. It distinguishes source liability, QDMTT and IIR assignment, UTPR assignment, SbS extinction and unsupported residual amounts. Shadow unsupported records are separate from receipts. The production Stage 06 allocator continues to stop on a positive pool with no supported collector. Stage 07a's preferred uncertainty, Stage 09's source-routing projections and Stage 11's sparse operator and games continue to use the frozen weights or rebuild them under the frozen policy. R05b must propagate an approved mapping through those paths and verify their distinct estimands.

Appendix E currently associates 6,587 with parent-years and 308 with parent-years requiring fallback. The frozen weight files actually contain 6,587 **parent × year × collector weight rows** and 308 **year × collector fallback weight rows**. The manuscript also moves from the group formula to parent-jurisdiction routing in the same paragraph. Section 6.1 and the limitations should distinguish this projection from legal group entitlements and separate the IIR and UTPR maps, including Cyprus and the Article 50 source exception. The manuscript remains at the frozen specification for R05a.
