# R06a. Safe-harbour legal audit

The policy comparison uses a fiscal year beginning **1 January 2026** and the R05b evidence cutoff of **15 November 2026**. The economic observations remain FY2017–2023. A rule's first applicable fiscal year, national commencement, substantive conditions and a group's election are separate facts.

## Permanent Simplified ETR Safe Harbour

The January 2026 Inclusive Framework package permits the Filing Constituent Entity to elect separately for each Tested Jurisdiction of its MNE group and fiscal year. A Tested Jurisdiction is the set for which GloBE requires a separate ETR calculation. Ordinary constituent entities and permanent establishments, separately tested JV groups, minority-owned groups and stateless entities require attention to their respective rules. Investment entities are generally ineligible as a separate Tested Jurisdiction, with specified elections and treatment for same-country investment entities. Top-up is deemed zero under the safe harbour where Simplified Taxes divided by Simplified Income is **at least 15%**, or where there is a Simplified Loss. The loss case has separate negative-tax-expense and carry-forward provisions that can require an adjustment or additional charge. Ordinarily, first election requires no top-up liability in the relevant fiscal years beginning within the preceding 24 months. Continued election can be annual. Re-entry after an interruption has a separate 24-month history test based on full GloBE or specified safe harbours. [OECD January 2026 package, chapter 2, Boxes 2 and 7.2 and paragraphs 177–188](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/side-by-side-package.pdf).

Simplified Income starts from the relevant financial-accounting net income or loss and adds back income tax expense to reach a pre-tax basis. It adjusts for items including excluded dividends, excluded equity gains or losses and specified transaction or other adjustments. Simplified Taxes starts from current and deferred income tax expense or benefit of the entities in the Tested Jurisdiction, then applies scope, allocation, timing and deferred-tax adjustments. The applicable consolidated or permitted local financial-accounting standard and domestic QDMTT accounting rules can matter. Public CbCR supplies aggregated profits, accrued and cash tax, and in some cases ETR brackets and activity. It lacks the entity and group identities, consolidation and blending data, covered tax and deferred tax adjustments, prior top-up history and elections needed to apply these provisions. A low-tail ETR proxy between 13% and 15% supplies none of the requisite eligibility evidence. [OECD January 2026 package, chapter 2, Boxes 3–5 and 7.1–7.3](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/side-by-side-package.pdf).

| Fiscal year begins | Availability under the agreed framework |
| --- | --- |
| Before 31 December 2025 | Outside the permanent SESH commencement provision |
| 31 December 2025 through 30 December 2026 | Optional early domestic implementation, subject to at least one of three conditions |
| From 31 December 2026 | General availability under the framework, subject to implementation, eligibility and election |

For early application, the implementing or QDMTT-only jurisdiction must make the safe harbour available. In addition, the QDMTT Safe Harbour must apply to the Tested Jurisdiction, a single jurisdiction must have GloBE taxing rights over that Tested Jurisdiction, or all jurisdictions with such rights must offer early application and the group must elect consistently in all of them. Thus the model's 1 January 2026 case falls in the optional period. Commission Notice C/2026/253 confirms application of the Inclusive Framework agreement in the context of Article 32 of the Directive, which provides for deemed zero on election subject to the agreement's conditions. Article 11 separately provides for a national QDMTT. Where a jurisdiction makes SESH available in its QDMTT, domestic liability can be affected subject to the applicable dates, accounting basis, substantive conditions and election. An applicable QDMTT Safe Harbour can facilitate early SESH, while the domestic tax still requires its own analysis. For a 2026 domestic relief model, obtain the operative national rule and applicable fiscal-year provisions, confirm any conditions on qualification or early application in each taxing jurisdiction, and establish group qualification and election. The R05b charging and collector register and the Commission notice do not supply this evidence. [OECD January 2026 package, chapter 2, Box 7.4 and paragraphs 196–197](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/side-by-side-package.pdf) · [Directive 2022/2523, Articles 11 and 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32022L2523) · [Commission Notice C/2026/253, 12 January 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=OJ%3AC_202600253).

## Implication for the paper

Section 4.4 assigns observations to the permanent safe harbour using the band. Appendix C attributes the notch itself to that rule. Both descriptions give the simulated band a legal interpretation that its inputs cannot support. In the code the band erases liability before the QDMTT, SbS, IIR and UTPR decision, including at QDMTT hosts and on amounts that would otherwise be extinguished by SbS. A fiscal comparison must therefore distinguish gross band exposure, otherwise collectible amounts by channel and the distinct effect of any subsequent screen.
