# R09a statutory CIT rate audit

The source-rate name `cit_top_all` is misleading. It stores the OECD central-government rate targeted at small businesses. In R06b it can govern a statistical ETR cap, a reconciliation covariate, an instrument and the pricing of ordinary CIT. These roles have different estimands. The existing evidence supports a standard combined rate as a headline policy covariate and a destination marginal-rate proxy. The combined series is also a defensible primary **statistical** cap input, provided the restriction is disclosed and tested separately from the mean. [Decision rules set before comparison](r09a/decision_rules.md) · [Machine-readable dependency inventory](r09a/dependency_inventory.csv).

## Source and vintage

The pinned extract is `data/raw/oecd_tax_rates/oecd_statutory_cit_rates.csv`. The acquisition code identifies dataflow `OECD.CTP.TPS:DSD_TAX_CIT@DF_CIT(2.0)`. The pinned file contains 12,210 records for 146 area codes in 2000–2023. Its last committed change was `4edd793` on 12 November 2026. The CSV has no observation-level publication timestamp. The input digest identifies the exact vintage. The live 2026 explanation describes subsequent editions and the Data Explorer displays observations through 2026. The live observation download returned HTTP 403, so overlapping-year revisions were not audited. The official live structure response was available and its codelists were checked. [Pinned input manifest](../../tests/step17_raw_inputs.sha256) · [Acquisition request](../../code/collect_all_data.py#L60) · [OECD SDMX structure and codelists](https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/dataflow/OECD.CTP.TPS/DSD_TAX_CIT@DF_CIT/2.0?references=all) · [OECD Data Explorer](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/vis?df%5Bag%5D=OECD.CTP.TPS&df%5Bds%5D=dsDisseminateFinalDMZ&df%5Bid%5D=DSD_TAX_CIT%40DF_CIT&df%5Bvs%5D=2.0).

| Measure / sector / targeting | Economic quantity |
|---|---|
| `CIT_C / S13 / ST` | Standard combined general-government CIT on taxable corporate income. Includes central and subcentral taxation as combined by the OECD, accounting for interactions such as deductions. |
| `CIT / S1311 / ST` | Standard central-government statutory CIT. |
| `CIT / S1311 / TA` | Central-government CIT targeted by small-business size criteria, including turnover, taxable income, assets and employment. |
| `CIT_SCG / S13M / ST` | Standard state and local component where reported. Report it separately. |
| `CIT_LSN / S1311 / ST` | Central-government rate less deductions for subnational taxes. |
| `CIT_EX_ST / S1311 / ST` | Central-government rate exclusive of surtax. |

Adding the subcentral component to the published combined rate would double count it. A standard headline rate and a small-business rate identify distinct categories even where a progressive schedule makes the headline a top-rate comparator. The general rate gives neither a firm's realised ETR nor its actual marginal rate after losses, credits or special regimes. [OECD Corporate Tax Statistics 2026, statutory rates](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/corporate-tax-statistics-2026_73af6222-en/full-report/statutory-corporate-income-tax-rates_ce84abb9.html) · [OECD Corporate Income Tax Rates Database](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/corporate-income-tax-rates-database.html).

`REF_AREA` and `TIME_PERIOD` identify the country and observation year. `FREQ=A` is annual. `PT_INC_TAX` is percentage of taxable income and `UNIT_MULT=0` gives units. Divide by 100 exactly once for model fractions. The pinned records all report two displayed decimals, with additional precision preserved where supplied. Dividend treatment, rate structure and tax base are `_Z` (not applicable). There is no separate taxpayer-category dimension in this flow. `OBS_STATUS=A` is a normal value and `I` marks imputation by the receiving agency. The 24 `I` observations are Colombia's targeted central series. They remain visible and retain their legacy role in the reference. An observed zero remains distinct from an absent observation and from an invalid supplied value. The worked fixture tests invalid numbers, units and duplicate keys, including a duplicate after `XKX` is normalised to `XKV`. The host-year joins never search a neighbouring year. [OECD codelists](https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/dataflow/OECD.CTP.TPS/DSD_TAX_CIT@DF_CIT/2.0?references=all) · [Tests](../../tests/testthat/test-statutory-rate-audit.R).

## Where the rate enters

Stage 01 pivots the central `CIT` measure. Stage 02 joins host and parent rates to the bilateral spine. It bounds the bilateral ETR **after** applying the profit correction, in order testing whether the targeted cap binds, then whether the standard central cap binds, then whether the unit ceiling binds. Its lower rule drops ratios below −5 percentage points and sets smaller negative ratios to zero. The regression ETR uses the corrected raw ratio with its independent support and winsorisation rules. Stage 03 clips the pooled mean at the preferred rate plus five points, with a unit ceiling, and also uses that rate to condition Table I knots. For L4, the raw percentile ranks and the conditioned knots enter different parts of the calculation. [Stage 02](../../code/02_base_correction_and_merging.R#L630) · [Stage 03](../../code/03_stage1_etr.R#L758) · [Numerical contract](../architecture/current_architecture.md#r08-constrained-beta-numerical-kernel).

In Stage 02a, the standard-rate substitution changes the parent-year covariate entering the weighted reconciliation prior. The statutory value in the public parity record is descriptive. The parity correction itself uses domestic and foreign tax-to-profit ratios. Stages 05 and 09 use the statutory series in robustness comparisons and as a pricing and eligibility input for ordinary CIT. The continuous response uses the estimated ETR gap and preferred coefficient. Stage 10 directly repeats the statistical rate ceiling when switching accounting numerators. Stages 06, 07a and 11 inherit changed liability, residual calibration or scenario inputs. Legal collector flags and weights, observed ordinary CIT denominators and budget amounts come from separate inputs. ## Ordinary CIT on fixed routes

Cumulative preferred SbS figures are US$bn. Gross repricing uses the same destination volumes, frozen route eligibility and existing formulary weights. Origin losses use the effective rate of the displaced low-tax profit. Applying a statutory rate to both ends is an explicitly separate stress test. This distinction matters because the low-tax origin and the normal-tax destination face different marginal tax conditions.

All US$1,653.382bn of global destination flow is priced at a supported standard rate in this fixed-route arm. About US$13.980bn of origin flow lacks a standard rate, or 0.85% of shifted profit. On covered origin flow, replacing the effective loss with the headline statutory rate prices US$329.424bn of lost CIT, illustrating why the two estimands should not be merged. Missing origin prices are separately flagged and the full unsupported price stays missing. Top-up liabilities and collector receipts are shared with the reference for the entire repricing exercise. Separately, a mechanical comparison of the statutory 15% gate finds 44 changed host-years over 2017–2023, including Canada, Switzerland, Chile, Costa Rica, Korea, Lithuania and Poland. This shows why repricing cannot stand in for a change of route eligibility. ## R09b decision memorandum

| Role | Recommended first implementation | Falsification or comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Observed ETR | Continue to derive it from observed accrued tax and the appropriate corrected profit denominator. Expose this ratio separately from regularised means. Keep the independent regression support and winsorisation. | Matched accounting observations and documented support changes can distinguish genuine tax timing from data errors. |
| Statistical mean and percentile support | Use same-year standard combined, then labelled standard central, then a unit ceiling for unsupported rates. Treat the existing five-point allowance as a transitional statistical convention and keep the existing negative rule. Targeted small-business taxation has no general support justification for the modelled large groups. | Test a unit-only upper bound and a small allowance grid on frozen S1/S2/S3, direct support and the L4 tail. Check substantive stability, rank conflict and missing fitted support. Prefer the definition justified by the estimand even where another arm gives lower MAE. |
| Reconciliation prior | Use the combined-first policy covariate for standard tax conditions, retain the observed parity and direct-profit evidence, and expose the model's missing covariate encoding in provenance and outputs. | Holdout and leave-covariate-out checks can show whether results depend on the statutory prior term or on its existing numerical zero encoding for missing input. A new missingness rule should be validated and identified separately. |
| Statutory regressor and instrument | Use the standard combined same-year measure and exact lag where the intended object is all-government statutory policy. Leave unavailable instruments missing. Preserve the simulated EATR series and the leading ETR-based estimation design. | Reproduce matched IV and natural support, inspect the first-stage strength and exclusion restrictions, and report the sign change. There is no empirical basis here to replace the leading coefficient with the statutory IV point estimate. |
| Ordinary CIT | Price destination profit at same-year combined-first as a transparent all-government marginal proxy. Use the effective low-tax origin loss as the central ledger and show a statutory-origin sensitivity separately. Keep unsupported prices visible. Separate the eligibility input from the pricing input. | Compare central-only pricing, the observed-ETR destination sensitivity and a separately identified destination-gate reform. Public CbCR cannot identify firm-specific relief, incremental effective taxation or realised collections. |
| Accounting and ancillary consumers | Pass the named measurement caps into the Stage 10 numerator variants. Keep observed CIT denominators and law separate from the selected statutory policy fields. | Cross the original and candidate rate definitions with the accounting switch. Refit the changed residual calibration and rerun repeal, routing and benchmark comparisons. |

The leading R09b candidate is the combined-first statistical measurement and reconciliation configuration evaluated here, subject to holdout and wider support checks. Keep the ordinary destination pricing revision and statutory IV reporting as separately identified effects within that change. Carry the existing model assumptions on observed and missing data through the first controlled comparison. Publish an attribution from cap, mean and reconciliation to the joint result and expose any interaction separately. Require complete old and new runs over the 32 and 64 designs, Stage 07a and joint draws, the repeal game, budget and fiscal exhibits, and manuscript Sections 3.2, 4.1–4.2, 4.4 and 6 and Appendices A–C as affected. A new scientific freeze belongs after the selected specification is reviewed. S02 and S10 remain scientific dependencies for later revisions. The conditional tests above preserve their current treatment.
