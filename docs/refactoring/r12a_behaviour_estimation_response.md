## Estimation sample and fit contract

The baseline uses FY2017–2023 and the existing `has_behavioral_estimation_inputs` flag with positive corrected profit, SBIE payroll, SBIE assets, affiliate population and GDP per capita, plus a nonmissing regression ETR. An eligible pair has at least two distinct years after those screens. Stale-wage, balanced-pair and 2020–2021 exclusions remain separate sample variants. The transformations are logs of the positive levels, the existing winsorisation of `etr_panelai_reg` and `tau_sq = tau^2`. The sample boundary screens the derived ETR. A missing original tax field with an available derived ETR is distinct from a missing ETR. Year and pair identifiers are kept as supplied, and no added deduplication, sort or zero fill is applied.

The first-stage statutory instrument uses the previous calendar year's host standard-combined CIT with declared missingness. Stage 09's current-year statutory-rate regressor is a separate exercise with 9,605 supplied rows. The lagged CbCR regressor joins the same bilateral pair at year minus one. The jackknife uses finite accrued tax and positive corrected profit donors, subtracts the focal parent before dividing, and requires at least two other foreign parents and positive donor profit. Its main donor pool can include the host's domestic cell. The separate foreign-only version also removes the domestic contribution. Both apply the original bounds and winsorisation. The matched samples select a common support for each proxy separately and compare current and alternative ETRs on that support. Neither a different proxy nor a matched comparison replaces the baseline sample.

The nonnegative PPML diagnostic can use a profit-weighted host-year ETR for zero-profit rows without an observed ETR. Its bounds come from the baseline sample and its fixed-effect removal is the original fixest behaviour. Log assets, payroll, GDP per capita and population are the four baseline controls. Stage 09 separately compares matched lagged activity for assets and payroll. The baseline fits use alternating within demeaning and QR column selection before `lm.fit`. The logarithmic offset search considers 60 values from .001 to .200, screens for a positive log argument greater than `1e-8` at the minimum ETR and takes the lowest BIC. The chosen offset is **0.15277966101694918**. Main manual BIC ranks are linear 43,681.866, logarithmic 43,684.392 and quadratic 43,687.680. Weighted Gaussian BIC uses its analytic-weight likelihood calculation; PPML BIC uses the Poisson objective. Cross-family BIC levels have different likelihood bases.

The manual covariance uses affiliate clustering and a separate parent-plus-affiliate inclusion–exclusion sandwich. Each component uses its own finite-cluster factor and the original FE rank correction. Reference degrees of freedom are affiliates minus one or the smaller cluster count minus one, with the original lower bound. The fixest diagnostic fits retain their own small-sample correction, including full FE count and conventional cluster adjustments. Rank-deficient manual slopes are dropped by QR, a collinear excluded instrument or fitted endogenous regressor stops the IV fit, and an absent/singular tax-term covariance block produces missing Wald inference. Negative manual variance gives a missing standard error. Manual fits record rank, objective and likelihood but do not export an iteration-convergence flag. Stage 05 alternative fits retain the original fixest convergence flag. Stage 09 additionally records repair flags and its existing affiliate-only Wald check for flagged cases. No variance repair is introduced into the manual models.

## Point coefficient, uncertainty and source response

The reported current linear slope is **−1.4651183078485985** per unit of the ETR fraction with two-way clustered SE **0.24295005765434916** and p **2.4199779698212027e−7** (47 reference degrees of freedom). The semi-elasticity is the negative derivative of log profit. The point model uses the exact signed coefficient in `step1_statutory_rate_specification.csv`, which is pinned at the R06b value via `applied_preferred_beta()`. The refitted numerical point happens to be identical here. The coefficient metadata and regression table serve different roles even when they agree. The Stage 05 host schedule and Stage 11 game, strict-ladder, accounting and joint paths apply that stored point on the leading profile. Stage 07a updates ETR exposure at the stored coefficients for measurement draws. Its profit-only exercise retains the preferred source schedule. Stage 11 uses the pinned point as the normal draw mean and the displayed two-way SE as its standard deviation. The original draw ordering and seeds (20260613, 20260503 and 20261113) remain in the orchestration. A PPML alternative, legacy notch or imposed-slope sensitivity has its own label.

The detailed schedule clips the observed low-tax rate τ to [0, m] for the minimum rate m = 0.15. For the linear model the log-profit change Δf is β₃(m − τ). The quadratic model adds β₄(m² − τ²) and the logarithmic model adds β₄[log(t₀ + m) − log(t₀ + τ)]. Retention is exp(Δf) bounded to [0, 1], and relocation share is one minus retention. Rates and shares are fractions and monetary fields are USD. The production threshold comes from `GMT_RATE` and the source cap from `BEHAVIOURAL_MAX_REALLOCATION_SHARE` (one). An available static schedule carries the bounded share and regime, with bandwidth zero in the leading specification.

A behavioural row needs positive post-substance top-up and positive low-tax excess profit to move profit on the continuous branch. A host QDMTT exposes the source even under SbS. A qualifying SbS exemption without a host QDMTT suppresses the continuous response. Positive IIR and potential UTPR are exposed, including a potential UTPR pool without an observed recipient. A missing or non-finite rate cannot select a low-tax response, and a non-finite supplied share maps to zero. The integrated exponent keeps its original boundary behaviour, including a saturated share of one for a negative infinite slope at a positive finite gap. At 15% there is no incremental response. The historical `BASELINE_NOTCH` zeroes top-up on the interval [0.13, 0.15) with zero relocation on that branch, independently of an actual SESH election. Other width diagnostics are separately labelled.

The source keeps the positive profit base, low-tax share, low-tax profit and excess profit separately. On the active continuous branch, moved profit is excess low-tax profit times the applied share. Implied retained excess profit is the difference. Remaining top-up is the original post-substance top-up times the liability filter, floored at zero, and avoided top-up is its nonnegative reduction. These operations use the existing amounts before and after the response, including the Factor 2 scale already applied to top-up. The diagnostic 72/28/0 channel split is fixed, and its three monetary components sum to moved profit. Ordinary source CIT enters the separate destination calculations. The Stage 06 waterfall checks assigned QDMTT/IIR/UTPR, SbS extinction and unsupported UTPR against the post-response source liability.

The sparse path retains its own numerically ordered expression for the linear exponent on measure-specific gaps, existing cap and route exposure, while sharing the regime classifier. It applies the resulting source vector to the existing collector operator. Source keys, 32 scenario identifiers, both Factor 2 modes and endpoint rates retain their original interpretation. The accounting and ETR ladder validators compare the two representations at equal grain with the documented US$0.001 collector-year contract. The 12 dense/sparse cases each cover 32 scenarios and 189 EU27 collector-years. They include both Factor 2 modes, L3, anchored and strict L4, adjusted L3 and L4 bases, and one joint draw. The maximum collector-year discrepancy is US$0.000038147. The recalculated comparison matches the R11c diagnostic exactly. [Evaluator checks](r12a/evaluator_parity.csv).

## Relocation destinations

`build_rule_b_reallocation()` and individual-source `allocate_rule_b_sources()` in Stage 05 source from scenario–parent–affiliate–year and candidate destination keys. Eligible same-parent destinations pass the historical targeted-first statutory gate, exclude the origin, have an ETR above the source and positive observed activity. The weights use one quarter employees, one quarter payroll and one half unrelated revenue, with renormalisation over eligible activity. The year-level global activity fallback retains the source-rate comparison. Positive sources still lacking destinations fail source-level conservation. The final Stage 05 matrix aggregates by scenario, year, destination and channel. Fine source keys are available before that aggregation.

The Stage 05 compatibility matrix also retains a distinct global Rule A variant. It pools by scenario, year and behavioural channel, then falls back to all positive-profit destinations if its high-tax non-conduit set is empty. Stage 09's `build_parent_specific_reallocation()` implements Rule A at scenario–parent–year pool grain. It excludes named conduit havens, applies the statutory gate and falls back to global-year weights. Its existing activity normaliser can use equal weights on an otherwise eligible zero-activity set. Rule A's profit-weighted source rate comes from the original source rows. `build_hub_destination_routes()` applies Rule B or the Rule C split. Rule C redirects half to the headquarters where the headquarters gate passes, including its separately recorded same-country cases. The rest follows the Rule B formulary route, with the full formulary route where the headquarters gate fails. Route and shadow identities compare allocated profit to the same source or parent pool, not to collected top-up.

`build_destination_comparison()` and `build_total_fiscal_country_year()` distinguish the historical gate from the leading standard-combined destination price with same-year standard-central fallback. The latter is a marginal-rate proxy. Current ordinary CIT loss at source applies the relevant low source ETR to moved profit. Net destination gain deducts this loss at the stated incidence grain. Destination effective-rate and historical-price variants remain separate. The full replay compares exported fiscal flows and destination matrices by exact bytes. R12b is the destination extraction boundary.

## IV interpretation and covariance evidence

The code computes the squared excluded-instrument first-stage t statistic under each covariance calculation, which is a one-restriction Wald F form. It derives the statistic from the manual model and does not request a generic fixest `ivf1`. The IV table reports the two-way-clustered values in its manuscript panel. [First-stage, reduced-form and 2SLS ledger](r12a/iv.csv).

| Excluded instrument | First-stage slope and statistic | 2SLS slope | Two-way SE | p | N |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lagged standard-combined CIT | +0.149775 and 2.088326 | +0.332294 | 3.351038 | 0.921432 | 9,603 |
| Simulated fixed-project EATR | +0.264032 and 10.812194 | −2.244902 | 1.556282 | 0.155942 | 8,820 |

The statutory rate has weak empirical relevance on its fitted support. Its positive 2SLS slope follows from a small positive reduced form and a positive first stage, with very wide uncertainty. R09b's switch from the historical rate to the standard combined rate changed the point sign on matched observations as first-stage relevance fell. That sensitivity provides little basis for a sign conclusion. The EATR's stronger point relevance and opposite reduced form operate on a different sample and carry no independent validation of exclusion. Statutory or simulated project taxes can affect profit location directly, correlate with fiscal reforms, and respond to endogenous activity and tax-base shocks. Lagging cannot remove persistent common shocks. Neither instrument demonstrates that the ETR denominator problem is resolved. Clustered first-stage diagnostics must be interpreted under the corresponding error and small-sample assumptions. R12c can study cluster-appropriate weak-IV-robust confidence sets and common-sample exclusion diagnostics. [Montiel Olea and Pflueger on robust relevance](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.2013.806694) · [Andrews, Stock and Sun on weak-IV practice](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-economics-080218-025643).

The reproducible Stage 08 warning arises during `fixest::feols()` with parent + affiliate clustered covariance, version **0.14.2**, for the EU27 host-pooled L3 liability-rate regression. The outcome is the initial Stage 1 L3 liability rate. Regressors are the entity shares for holding, IP, intra-group finance, R&D and manufacturing. The fit has separate affiliate and year effects, 2,689 bilateral rows for 2017–2023, 40 parent clusters, 27 affiliate clusters and 26 inference degrees of freedom. The audit reruns the same fit and then requests the same unrepaired matrix with `vcov_fix = FALSE`. Its lowest eigenvalue is **−6.5585344e−7**, compared with **9.9998213e−17** after repair. This is a genuinely indefinite matrix, consistent with the subtraction term in multiway-cluster inference. [Cameron, Gelbach and Miller](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.07136).

Holding activity's coefficient is +0.005203883. Its SE changes from 0.002260506 to 0.002358759, unadjusted p from 0.029592 to 0.036408 and Holm p from 0.118369 to 0.145632. For R&D the SE changes from 0.004166238 to 0.004183940, with Holm p moving from 0.092318 to 0.094570. Every coefficient, repaired SE and repaired p-value matches the archived diagnostic within `1e-12` absolute on the exported scales. The raw p-values only illustrate the sensitivity of the diagonal-based calculations. A negative eigenvalue makes the unrepaired matrix unsuitable as a general covariance estimate.
