**Set-up of document of research proposal**

- **Title page**: Proposal for Master Thesis U.S.E., title of research, student name contact, supervisor
- **Introduction**: where is paper about (first sentence!), what is problem and why relevant (to whom), short literature review, derive and motivate research question (plus sub questions), explain contribution, announce and motivate research question
- **Literature review and theoretical framework**: Explain the proposed theory, Define the main theoretical concepts based on the available literature and explain their relevance, Explain relevant concepts in detail; provide bird’s eye view of past relevant research, If applicable formulate hypotheses, propositions, or conceptual model; include detailed, arguments for these (avoid throwing randomly formulated hypotheses to paper!), Expect you need to cover at least 20 sources (mostly academic papers; start reading early on in period 3 and check with your supervisor if you have an appropriate initial list of relevant sources) to be able to cover the items above.
- ***Methodology and empirical strategy**: describe and motivate the proposed methods of data collection and the kind of data that will be collected, sampeling strategy if neeeded, operationalisation (how will the concepts be measured), proposed methods of data anlaysis, expected types of outcomes
- **Activities and timeline**: include timetable from April-end of June (period 4), list of activities leading up to answering the research question (think backward from final results to data collection), milestone for each activity: when is it finished?, include time for writing draft versions of the result and discussion sections, meetings to discuss them with the university supervisor, ample time (a week) for collecting feedback, rewriting, editing and final revisions

        - JEL codes that fit thesis: https://www.aeaweb.org/econlit/jelCodes.php?view=jel
        - some keywords that fit thesis for search engine
        - footnote to the title gives any acknowledgements of financial or other support and gives thanks to people that have supported the writing of the thesis. Conclude footnote with: ‘The copyright of this thesis rests with the author. The author is responsible for its contents and opinions expressed in the thesis. U.S.E. is only responsible for the academic coaching and supervision and cannot be held liable for the content
    - **Introduction**: that is thesis about, what is the relevance to reader (motivated), brief and critical literature review of peer reviewed, scientific knowledge, 1 to 2 paragraphs, only most relevant, passive citing to describe where consensus already lies, what should we want to know = research questions (one or more gaps in the academic knowledge), how would knowing that be relevant to the reader (zoom in on the most relevant gap), how can we break-up, research and answer that question in a logical way (subquestions that answer the main RQ) what are expected results to be found and how do they contribute the the academic literature, what is the structure of the paper 
    - **Literature review**: critical review of relevant theories and empirical research from availiable literature in books and journals, 
        - What are we looking for? What parts of RQ, method, topic, theory, evidence, dependent variable or independent variables
        - Waht is main argument/result in paper 
        - Which detail level is needed
        - What is main take away of paper? In own words!
        - Aim for 70% or more peer reviewed journals, good institutions and governments, only news for showing relevance
        - Position literature review in high-quality, high-impact literature
        - Answer following elements: possibilities and limitations of existing literature, why and how different schools of thought and reasoning differ, what is irrespective of differences the broad consensus, what is central argument and how does it relate to literature, what is difference to existing arguments 
        - Ignore irrelevant literature, evaluation is fair, space is created for own argument
        - Mistakes often made: Connection between own theory and existing literature is unclear, Not the topic, but the argument is decisive for the literature selection, Limitation of the literature against the background of the analysis, It is not about reproducing all published works in the field. Only those papers that illustrate the gap! Do not just report, argue!
    - **Theoretical framework** (can be combined with literature review), no need to develop a new theoretical framework for master thesis (although allowed for higher grade of course), as in many theses it suffices to apply and use existing theory. propose own contribution to the literature (by a general theoretical framework that explains relaitonships expected and analysis of the problem): what you expect to be the most important causal relationships that explain the phenomenon you study, look for papers that have studied individual links in that causal chain and see what theoretical frameworks they propose,  X influences Y (if applicable, Z), theory answers why does this apply when does it apply (probabilistic context, deterministic relationship), 
        - If understand the causal mechanisms behind the phenomen, infomration to change the situation and imrpove the outcome, convice the reader that this theory actually explains the facts and relevant in the given context. Put the theory to the test. "A testable hypothesis is a prediction we can make using the assumptions of the model and logic. Assuming A, B and C and given Z, an increase in X causes an increase in Y"
        - **Hypothesis development**: 
            1. Redefine central argument
            2. Intuition behind argument (make reader understand argumentative development of hypothesis)
            3. Objective functions of actors and framework conditions
            4. causal relationship present in main relationships and individual hypothesis
            5. explanatory power of own theory in comparison to other theories
            6. causal relationships must be persent in main relationship and the individual hypothesis
            7. put explanatory power of own theory in perspection to other theories
        - Add diagrams to explain theory. Formalisation force to make tracable. Theory should by cristal clear. 
        - Be precise and consise
        - And theoretical section with set of carefully derived and related, testable hypothesis
    - **Emprirical strategy** (data collection, description): 
        - how your variables are defined empirically and present descriptive statistics
        - Replicability of the analysis is the most important criterion for the quality of scientific research.
        - Replicability distinguishes scientific texts from written expressions of opinion. Simplifications and generalizations of observed relationships (inferences) must be motivated and understandable!
        - reference to data, why that data, how reliable data, variable definition, apply existing measures, sources of measurs, why query, how data standarlised mathematically, which statistical proceedures, how missing information handled
        - present and discuss descrtiptive data
        - data collection and empirical methodfs 
    - **Results and interpretation**: 
        - data analysis: choice of empirical tools and model. Look at examples of papers that propose a similar approach (could be in different context), argue why the chosen empirical approach is the best one to address the research question and hypotheses you proposed with the data you collected
        - good robsustness checks
        - reports results on the derived hypotheses, predictions and implications that concluded the theoretical framework section by applying the methods on the data described in the empirical strategy section
        - only present main results
        - excellent figures and graphs
        - results of (sensible) robustness tests in footnotes as a rule and only report them in the main text if they are really important/different
        - Good robustness tests are motivated. Never just throw in a few mechanically fabricated alternative specifications and call that a robustness test. Think about what alternative explanations may cause your results and try to design tests that rule these alternative explanations out.
        - Describe all tables you have filled, graphs you presented and other representations of the data you include in the main text. Do not use place holders and put graphs and tables behind your text in the final thesis.
    - **Discussion and conclusion**: interpretation of (the significance of) your research approach and results for academia and practice.
        - four ingredients: discussion, implications, limitations, conclusion
        - findings should be interpreted in the light of previous research. Thus, in the discussion section to first position your results in the extant academic literature to carve out the contribution of your thesis. Limit this discussion to a few key publications already discussed in the introduction. Do not introduce new material. In the discussion you should consistently refer back to your theory and empirical findings, in order to  relate your empirical outcomes to already existing insights. Also, make recommendations to other academic researchers about logical next steps, and provide suggestions for future research
        - important to reflect on the implications of your research for practitioners i.e. non- academics. Why should managers, policy-makers, decision-makers care? What is it that they can take away from your thesis?
        -  limitations. No academic study is perfect, and how is the time to acknowledge weaknesses of your study
        - conclusion: formulate an answer to the main research question(s); • summarise the main argument and its theoretical and practical relevance; • discuss the validity and reliability of findings and arguments; • nuance the conclusions and recommendations if appropriate.
    - **References**: APA 7th edition
    - **Appendices**: auxiliary/helpful for the reader to follow and reconstruct what you have done, but that is not essential to follow the main line of argumentation of your paper,  all the tables, graphs, figures, transcripts, screen shots and what not that help the reader understand details he may want to consult, if you do not refer to it in the main manuscript, it does not belong in the appendix but should be put in supplementary material

## Factors taken into account in grading process

- **Societal relevance**: consistency of aim of research and question, proposed main research question and logical subquestions, connection to societal challenges. Demarcation, contextualisation, accountability of topic that leads to clear and relevant aim 
- **Scientific relevance**: describe anticipated contribution to existing literature, literature made applicable to own research, extend and relevancce of studied literature is outstanding, independent critical and in-depth review on literature, anticipated contribution to literature clearly specified, coherence between research question and anticipated contribution to literature/hypotheses
- **Methodology**: excellent research approach for research question, accountability of data collection and analysis, systematic operationalisation of research question, validity and reliability of study
- **Research approach** and implementation: one of most important parts: adequate quantitative analysis, adequate hypothesis testing or derivation of propositions, complete and to the point presentation of results
- **Interpretation of findings**: most important, answer based on literature and empirical/theoretical research, confrontation and intelligent combination of theoretical insights and empirical data, reflection on results via practical significance and policy recommendations
- **General presentation**: clear and functional structuring of thesis, appealing layout, grammatically correct and very good British English, accountability of sources and quotes
- **Conducting research in independent and self-responsible way**: independent, dealing with feedback, ingenuity, daring and relecance, performance in meetings

# Writing style

- Never write more or less than you need to make your point.
- master thesis should be written in good, active, clean and simple academic English. Be proud of your work and humble about its implications. The reader is always right. If the reader fails to get your brilliant ideas, it is your responsibility to improve your text. You need to decide on what you want to say and convince/seduce the reader to pay attention and read on.
- Every paragraph starts with a sentence summarizing its content.
- mix short and long sentences
-  Avoid sentences with sub-clauses, Avoid comma if you can use period, Avoid auxillary words, Avoid repetition of the same or similar terms, Avoid fill words that do not affect the content, Avoid technical jargon of the discipline
- avoid plagarism at all costs, in my .md notes I have written many times in the same language as the article or summarised by copying the most important parts of a sentence, including this again is still plagaraism. So avoid this and write in new language.
