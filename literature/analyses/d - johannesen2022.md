p.1
How will GMT shape national tax policies and welfare in formal model of international tax competition with heterogenous countries?

* Non-havens: higher welfare due to lower profit shifting $\rightarrow$ higher government revenue
* Lower welfare due to lower equilibrium tax in havens $\rightarrow$ transfers resources from non-haven firms to haven governments
* Positive net welfare effect for tax havens if GMT is so high that profit shifting ends.

Introduction: higher cross-border mobility of profits $\rightarrow$ cut corporate tax rates

GMT effect in countries affected by profit shifting:

* Increase tax payments from firms that shift profits to tax havens
* Firms induced to reduce profit shifting $\rightarrow$ boost government revenue and stop resource waste in tax planning
* Ease tax rate competition between countries $\rightarrow$ profits taxed at higher rate

Advantages of formal model over casual analysis:

* firm behavior
* national tax policies as endogenous to changes in international tax system
  $\rightarrow$ big role for other countries, since GMT could lead to a coordinated tax rate increase in havens $\rightarrow$ negative welfare effect for non-havens

Behavior: governments choose tax policies to maximise national welfare in context of international tax architecture (i.e., GMT rate)

* MNEs maximise global after-tax profits

Formal analysis:

1. Determine non-cooperative equilibrium in CIT with/without GMT
2. Identify how GMT affects equilibrium welfare

p.2
Without GMT: policy equilibrium zero tax rate in haven, positive tax rate in non-haven (since balance social cost and benefit of taxation via profit shifting opportunities by haven)

Higher rate $\rightarrow$ mechanical transfer from firm owners to government (potential equity gain)
& also leads to profit shifting (efficiency loss)

GMT $\rightarrow$ new policy equilibrium $\rightarrow$ havens adjust to GMT rate (floor on race-to-the-bottom)

Marginal increase in GMT rate affects welfare in non-havens via:

1. Lower welfare by raising equilibrium rate in havens $\rightarrow$ higher total tax liabilities for firms, less of private consumption for owners $\rightarrow$ lower welfare in non-havens
2. Raise welfare by narrowing tax differential between havens and non-havens $\rightarrow$ deterring profit shifting

Higher government revenue $\rightarrow$ gain of public consumption in non-havens $\rightarrow$ higher welfare
All other welfare effects cancel out by application of envelope theorem

Net effect of raising GMT on welfare in non-havens: ambiguous, more positive when profit shifting responds strongly to changes in tax incentives & government has low weight on marginal private consumption of firm owners.

Private consumption of firm owners: zero social value $\rightarrow$ positive welfare effect, since decrease to private consumption of firm owners has no weight in welfare function

Increasing social weight of firm owners $\rightarrow$ shifted profits need to be more elastic for reform to be welfare improving.

Private consumption of firm owners has same social value as public funds $\rightarrow$ semi-elasticity of profit shifting is 4

Conclusion: welfare effect is unambiguously positive when GMT rate sufficiently high
$\rightarrow$ no profit shifting and non-havens have first-best allocation, since:

* sum of private and public consumption is maximal due to no resources spent on profit shifting and havens capture no revenue
* split between private and public consumption fully efficient since tax instrument not associated with marginal DWL $deadweight loss$

Background:

p.3 Model: world with $N$ countries, firms conduct economic activities in non-havens, shift some profits to havens, households own firms and consume net profits via dividend, governments tax profits and use revenue to provide public goods.

Sequence:

1. Governments simultaneously choose domestic policies by taking GMT and anticipate firm responses to policy choice
2. Firms choose allocation of profits (easy as plus dimension of tax rate)
   $\rightarrow$ search Nash equilibrium in non-cooperative game played by governments
3. How equilibrium welfare of non-haven countries is shaped by GMT rate

Firms: only choice is where to report profits for tax purposes, concealment costs in countries where under-report profits

$\bar{\pi}*{ij}$: gross profit that firm based in $i$ derives from activities in $j$; losses are impossible, $\bar{\pi}*{ij} \ge 0$ in non-haven, $\bar{\pi}_{ij}=0$ in haven, some profits earned in each non-haven

$\pi_{ij}$: profits that firm based in country $i$ chooses to report for tax purposes in country $j$

Assume: reported profit = true total profit

$\Delta_{ij} \equiv \max$0, \bar{\pi}*{ij} - \pi*{ij}$: under-report in country $j$ by firm based in $i$

$S$\Delta_{ij}$: concealment cost, convex function with $S$0$=0$
$\Rightarrow$ increasingly costly for firms to underreport, no costly effort needed to conceal overreporting

$\Pi_i^{*}$: maximised total profits of firm in country $i$ net of taxes and concealment costs, both paid out to shareholders as dividends in proportion to ownership shares

Lemma 1: No GMT, $t_L$ is lowest tax rate
Country $j$ where $t_j > t_L$: optimal underreporting given by $\Delta_{ij}^{*} \le \bar{\pi}*{ij}$, under condition:
$S'(\Delta*{ij}^{*}) = t_j - t_L$ or $\Delta_{ij}^{*} = \bar{\pi}*{ij}$

p.5
Lemma 2: GMT at $t_m$ and top-up tax applies

$t_L > t_m$ $\rightarrow$ Lemma 1 applies $\rightarrow$ GMT not binding and $t_L$ lowest tax rate obtainable

$t_L \le t_m$ $\rightarrow$ Lemma 1 applies with $t_L$ replaced by $t_m$, since:
FOC: $\mu_i + \lambda_{ij} = \max$t_m, t_j$ - S'$\Delta_{ij}$ for all $ij$

Overreporting in $L$: $\mu_i = \max t_m, t_L$

Meaning: GMT puts floor on effective tax rate obtainable through cross-border profit shifting

If $t_L < t_m$: $t_m$ is lowest obtainable tax rate
$a$ $t_m$ is governing amount of underreporting chosen in high tax rate countries
$b$ all countries with tax rate $t_m$ and not just $t_L$ benefit from overreporting

Governments: NE where sets all governments set tax rate to maximise national welfare given tax rates set by all other governments

Proposition 1: No GMT, NE Nash equilibrium set:

* Havens set tax rate equal to 0
* Non-havens set tax rate based on:
  $$
  \frac{W_g^i}{W_c^i} = \frac{\Omega}{1+\varepsilon}
  $$
  with $\varepsilon = \dfrac{d \sum_j \pi_{ij} / \sum_j \pi_{ij}}{dt_i/t_i}$

and $\Omega = \theta_{ii}\pi_{ii}/\sum_j \pi_{ij} =$ share of profits reported in country $i$ owned by local households

$W_g^i$ = marginal social value of public good
$W_c^i$ = marginal social value of dividend payments

Meaning: shift profits to lowest tax rate $\rightarrow$ havens compete tax rate to zero and earn no revenue in equilibrium Bertrand competition

In NE: no individual haven can increase revenue and thus improve welfare by changing tax rate given zero tax rate set by other havens

p.5
P.5 FOC for optimal tax rate in non-haven by differentiating welfare function wrt tax rate:
$$
\int \Psi (\cdot) \left W_c^i \frac{dc}{dt} + W_g^i \frac{dg}{dt}\right  f$\alpha$, d\alpha = 0
$$

Small change in tax rate affects private consumption of all households who own shares and size of public good, thus:
$$
W_c^i -\Omega_i \pi_{ii}  + W_g^i\left \sum_j \pi_{ij} + \frac{d\pi_{ii}}{dt_i} t_i\right = 0
$$

$underbrace labels in notes:$

* $dc/dt$: negative welfare effect working through lower private consumption of firm owners
* $dg/dt$: positive welfare effect working through higher government revenue; $dg/dt$ depends on mechanical effects and profit shifting response

Revenue loss in $i$ due to taxation $\Rightarrow$ profit shifting requires socially wasted concealment

Proposition 2: With GMT at rate $t_m$, 2 policy choices will lead to NE:

* All havens set tax rate to $t_m$: dominates all other tax rates, by raising more gov’t revenue for same level of private consumption
* Non-havens satisfy $\dfrac{W_g^i}{W_c^i} = \dfrac{\Omega}{1+\varepsilon}$

Proposition 3: GMT at $t_m$ that sufficiently high $\rightarrow$ non-havens set tax rate that satisfies:
$$
\frac{W_g^i}{W_c^i} = \frac{\Omega}{\max}
$$
$\rightarrow$ which is NE together with any choice in tax rates in havens

p.6 mixed equilibrium where some non-havens choose $t_m$, others choose interior optimum, and number of non-havens choose interior optimum

Welfare Analysis
2 distorting factors for policy choice of non-havens:

* mobility of profits $\varepsilon$ raises marginal cost of public funds above true social cost
* tax exporting created by cross-border ownership of profits $\Omega$ lowers cost of public funds below true social cost

Assumption 1: all profits accrue to households: $\Omega = 1$

Proposition 4: GMT at low rate in non-havens: lowers welfare by increasing firms’ foreign tax costs and increases welfare by mitigating erosion of domestic tax base through profit shifting

Positive welfare effect when private consumption of firm owners has low marginal weight in social welfare function $W_c^i$\cdot$ small relative to $W_g^i$\cdot$

Small increase in GMT lowers welfare by increasing firms’ tax liability in havens

Proposition 5: GMT at sufficiently high rate $\rightarrow$ positive effect in non-havens
Since no profit shifting anymore $\rightarrow$ raises welfare via higher sum of private and government consumption no waste of resources on concealment and split between private and government consumption is fully efficient

Proposition 6: GMT that’s not high enough to eliminate profit shifting has positive effect on welfare in havens
