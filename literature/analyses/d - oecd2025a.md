# OECD 2025


## Section A Country-by-Country Reports: General Background

Q1 What are CbCRs?

CbCR was implemented under Action 13 of the OECD/G20 BEPS Project. Applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenues above EUR 750 million, who must annually submit CbCRs, typically in their headquarter jurisdiction. CbCRs contain aggregate information per jurisdiction on: global allocation of MNE income and taxes paid, indicators of the location of economic activity, which entities operate in each jurisdiction, and the nature of their business activities.

Q2 Purpose of CbCRs?

Primary purpose: support tax administrations in "high-level detection and assessment of transfer pricing and other BEPS-related risks." Secondary purpose: support economic and statistical analysis of BEPS behaviour and MNEs more generally.

Q3 Are CbCRs public?

No. In 2015, no consensus was reached to make individual CbCRs public. They are submitted to governments and kept confidential, consistent with general treatment of taxpayer information. However, under Action 11 of the BEPS Project, jurisdictions agreed to regularly publish anonymised and aggregated CbCR statistics.



## Section B Anonymised and Aggregated CbCR Statistics

Q4 What is the anonymised and aggregated CbCR dataset?

Provides global information on MNE activities at a more granular level than consolidated financial accounts. Includes data on: employees, related and unrelated party revenues, profits, taxes paid. Key features: covers all global activities of included MNEs; allows domestic and foreign activities to be separately identified; geographically disaggregated (level of detail differs by reporting jurisdiction); reported by jurisdiction of tax residence, not incorporation. Also provides cross-country information on MNEs' major business activities (e.g. manufacturing, IP holding, sales).

Q5 Why publish this data?

BEPS Action 11 Final Report (2015) found that BEPS analysis is "severely constrained by the limitations of currently available data." Other available data lacked comprehensive cross-jurisdictional and company coverage, and often did not include actual taxes paid. Members of the Inclusive Framework agreed to publish anonymised and aggregated CbCR statistics to "improve the measurement and monitoring of BEPS."



## Section C Data Collection and Coverage

Q6 How is the data collected?

Two-step process. First, all large MNEs file CbCRs with the tax administration in the jurisdiction of their ultimate parent entity (UPE). Second, each jurisdiction's tax administration (or other government body) compiles all filings into a single anonymised and aggregated dataset (according to its own confidentiality standards), which is then submitted to the OECD for consolidation. MNE groups are typically required to file their CbCR one year after the closing date of their fiscal year. The OECD has developed a common reporting template in collaboration with Inclusive Framework members.

Q7 Coverage and scope of the 2025 release?

The 2025 release covers fiscal year 2022, based on data provided by 54 jurisdictions covering over 8,700 MNEs worldwide. Coverage applies only to MNE groups with consolidated revenues above EUR 750 million (or near-equivalent in local currency as at January 2015). Coverage is complete for jurisdictions where CbCR filing was mandatory; likely incomplete where filing was voluntary.

Q8 Why not all Inclusive Framework members?

Of the over 140 Inclusive Framework members, 106 accepted CbCRs for fiscal year 2022. Of those, only 54 submitted data for publication, representing an estimated 98% of all CbCRs filed worldwide. Reasons for non-submission include: data quality concerns, technical difficulties, legal issues, or choosing to withdraw submissions. Five jurisdictions reported receiving zero CbCRs for fiscal year 2022.



## Section D Data Limitations

Q9 What are the main limitations?

Several important limitations are flagged in the official disclaimer:

Data is too aggregated for detailed investigation of specific BEPS channels notably, there is no distinction between royalties and interest in related party payments, and no information on intangible assets. CbCRs are often based on financial accounting data rather than tax reporting data, which may affect accuracy and cross-jurisdictional comparability. Several jurisdictions have not submitted data to the OECD. MNEs may have included intra-company dividends in profit figures, potentially introducing a downward bias in calculated effective tax rates (ETRs). For stateless income, inclusion of transparent entities such as partnerships may cause double-counting of revenue and profit, though conversely the data may imply that stateless profit is untaxed (whereas it is typically taxed at the level of the owner). CIT-exempt entities such as pension funds or university hospitals are included in the aggregated statistics (unless otherwise specified), which may distort the relationship between profits and taxes. Comparability between 2016 data and subsequent years is limited due to the transition from voluntary to mandatory filing and differences in fiscal year coverage.

Q10 Impact of including intra-company dividends?

In the absence of specific guidance, some MNEs included intra-company dividends in "profit (loss) before income tax," causing potential double-counting. This is standard in separate financial accounting but problematic in corporate tax analysis. Since repatriated dividends are often lightly taxed or exempt, their inclusion in pre-tax profit "can result in artificially low effective tax rates (ETRs)." Several jurisdictions have independently analysed this issue: the Netherlands, Ireland, Italy, Sweden, and the United Kingdom (footnote 1 provides direct links to each country's analysis).

Q11 How will limitations be addressed over time?

The CbCR implementation guidance was updated in November 2019 to specify that intra-company dividends should be excluded from profit figures. However, due to time lags in instructions and reporting, the issue may persist until statistics for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2020. The treatment of stateless entities is still under discussion, including through the 2020 Review of CbCR (BEPS Action 13). The OECD guidance on preparation of anonymised and aggregated statistics was updated in 2024 to ensure entities classified as "Stateless/Unattributable" are treated separately from domestic or foreign entities.



## Section E Anonymised and Aggregated CbCR Data and BEPS

Q12 What can the data tell us about BEPS?

Significant caveats apply to drawing BEPS conclusions from this data. Short-term comparability between 2016 and later years is limited (voluntary-to-mandatory filing transition; differences in fiscal year coverage note: 2016 covered CbCRs with fiscal years ending between 31 December 2016 and 30 June 2017, while subsequent releases cover 1 January to 31 December; see footnote 2). Much of the published data pre-dates the implementation of the OECD/G20 BEPS Package measures, as well as the US tax reform. Aggregation prevents investigation of specific BEPS channels. Financial accounting basis may not reflect tax reporting. Even with CbCR data, "it will still be difficult to fully separate BEPS from real economic activity and from non-BEPS tax preferences." Notwithstanding these caveats, the 2025 release suggests "some preliminary insights that could be indicative of BEPS."

Q13 What preliminary insights does the 2025 CbCR release offer?

Five key observations, all flagged as preliminary and subject to the data limitations above:

There is "a misalignment between the location where profits are reported and the location where economic activities occur." In investment hubs (defined as jurisdictions with total inward FDI above 150% of GDP), MNEs report on average 18% of profits, but only 4% of employees and 12% of tangible assets.

Revenues and profits per employee are markedly higher in investment hubs. For fiscal year 2022, the median revenues per employee in investment hubs was USD 1,729,000, versus USD 335,000 for all other jurisdictions. The OECD notes this "could reflect some commercial considerations" but is also "likely to indicate the existence of BEPS practices."

Related party revenues (transactions between entities controlled by the same MNE) account for 30% of total revenues at the median in investment hubs, compared to approximately 13% in high, middle, and low income jurisdictions.

The predominant business activity in investment hubs is "holding shares and other equity instruments," whereas sales, manufacturing, and services dominate in high, middle, and low income jurisdictions. A concentration of holding companies is identified as "a risk assessment factor and could be evidence of certain tax planning structures."

High-level BEPS indicators in investment hubs have declined relative to five years prior: median profits per employee fell by 19.3% relative to its 2017 value; median related party revenues as a share of total revenue fell by 15.8%. These declines could indicate reduced BEPS behaviour, though the OECD notes the 2022 data "could continue to be affected by the COVID-19 crisis."
