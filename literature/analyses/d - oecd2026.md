## Foreword (pp. 4–5)

- October 2021: IF members agreed a two-pillar solution in response to challenges of digitalisation. GloBE Rules are "designed to ensure large MNEs pay a minimum level of tax on the income arising in each jurisdiction where they operate."
- GloBE Rules have the status of a "common approach": jurisdictions are not required to adopt them, but if they do, they must implement consistently with agreed outcomes and must accept application by other members, including safe harbours and rule order.
- GloBE Model Rules approved 20 December 2021. The system consists of an IIR (Income Inclusion Rule) and UTPR (Undertaxed Profits Rule), designed to be compatible with the UN Model Double Tax Convention (2021) and OECD Model Tax Convention (2017).
- Commentary first approved 14 March 2022 – intended to promote consistent interpretation and promote certainty for MNE Groups.
- Administrative Guidance (AG) issued by the IF is incorporated into the Commentary once agreed.

This AG will be incorporated into the Commentary to the GloBE Model Rules.



## Chapter 1 – Side-by-Side Package (pp. 6–9)

### Section 1: Introduction (p. 6)

- The IF recognises the GMT as "a substantial policy initiative which plays an important role in stabilising the international tax system."
- The package consists of: (1) material simplifications, (2) greater alignment of substance-based tax incentives with QRTCs, and (3) a Side-by-Side (SbS) system.
- Agreed "in the context of an unwavering commitment to address substantial level playing field risks along with risks of BEPS."

### Section 2: Material Simplifications (pp. 6–7)

2.1. Simplified ETR Safe Harbour
- A "permanent Simplified ETR Safe Harbour" – ETR determined via a simple calculation based on income and taxes drawn from the MNE Group's reporting packages with minimal adjustments.
- Available from the beginning of 2027, or from beginning of 2026 in certain circumstances.

2.2. Extension of the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour
- Extended by one year to allow transition to the SESH.
- MNE Groups may choose between SESH and TCSH during the transition period.

2.3. Work programme for additional simplification
- Ongoing work on a routine profits test and de minimis test, scheduled to conclude in first half of 2026.
- Work on further simplification of GloBE Rules themselves, focus on continuity issues (ensuring taxpayers benefit from simplifications even in years when they do not qualify for the safe harbour).
- Further AG on technical issues.
- Possible integration of simplified calculations in the SESH into the design of the GMT itself, especially for lower capacity jurisdictions.
- Reporting: the GIR XML Schema and related validation rules will be adapted for agreed safe harbours, to be concluded in first half of 2026.

### Section 3: Substance-based Tax Incentives (p. 7)

- SBTI Safe Harbour allows MNE Groups to treat certain Qualified Tax Incentives (QTIs) as an addition to Covered Taxes.
- QTI = generally available tax incentive, calculated based on expenditures incurred (expenditure-based) or on amount of tangible property produced (production-based).
- Substance Cap limits the allowance for QTIs: the cap equals the greater of 5.5% of payroll costs or depreciation of tangible assets in the jurisdiction.
- Alternative cap (elective): 1% of the carrying value of tangible assets in the jurisdiction.

### Section 4: Side-by-Side System (pp. 8–9)

- The IF considers a co-ordinated GMT as the "primary system," but acknowledges some jurisdictions already have minimum taxation requirements for domestic and foreign income.
- SbS and UPE Safe Harbours apply to MNE Groups headquartered in jurisdictions the IF has determined to meet requirements for an eligible tax regime.
- SbS Safe Harbour: requires UPE in a jurisdiction with both an eligible domestic tax regime AND an eligible worldwide tax regime. When elected, no IIR or UTPR applies to that MNE Group.
- UPE Safe Harbour: applies only to jurisdictions meeting the domestic eligibility criteria. Provides a safe harbour for domestic profits; no UTPR applies to UPE jurisdiction profits.
- Qualified SbS or UPE Regime jurisdictions will be listed on the "Central Record."
- Assessment of eligibility by end of first half 2026 for jurisdictions requesting by that date; requests in 2027/2028 assessed timely on the same basis.
- All MNE Groups (including those eligible for SbS/UPE safe harbours) remain subject to QDMTT in all QDMTT jurisdictions. QDMTT calculated without pushdown of CFC or foreign branch taxes.

4.1. Stocktake
- A stocktake will be undertaken pursuant to an evidence-based objective process, to be agreed by the IF and concluded by 2029.
- The stocktake will consider: QDMTT implementation, competitive imbalances, negative taxpayer behaviours (e.g. inversions, material increase in profits in low-tax jurisdictions without QDMTTs).
- IF commits to take action to address any substantial identified risks to level playing field or BEPS.

4.2. Reinforcing effectiveness of QDMTTs
- QDMTTs continue to apply to all MNE Groups. Calculated without pushdown of CFC or other owner-level taxes.
- All IF members committed to crediting QDMTTs on same terms as other creditable foreign income taxes.
- Conditional or discriminatory taxes will not be recognised as Covered Taxes.
- Qualified status of QDMTTs remains dependent on consistent and non-discriminatory application regardless of whether an MNE Group has elected the SbS Safe Harbour.



## Chapter 2 – Simplified ETR Safe Harbour (pp. 10–67)

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 10–13)

Background
- In many jurisdictions, MNE Groups have ETRs exceeding 15%, so Top-up Tax liabilities are concentrated in a limited number of jurisdictions. Requiring full GloBE compliance where no Top-up Tax is due creates unnecessary compliance cost.
- The Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour (TCSH) provided temporary relief. Business at OECD (BIAC) approached the IF with a proposal for a permanent alternative.
- The IF worked with BIAC's Business Advisory Group on Pillar Two to develop a SESH that "incorporates the key simplifications identified by BIAC in a way that does not give rise to integrity concerns."
- Like other safe harbours, the SESH deems Top-up Tax in a jurisdiction to be zero when it applies.

Design overview (see Figure 1.1 on p. 11 of document):
- Reliance on financial accounts: calculated based on CFS; QDMTT jurisdictions encouraged to allow CFS.
- Jurisdictional approach rather than Constituent Entity-by-CE.
- Can rely on TP policy when accounts are in line with tax return.
- Generally do not need to remove PPA.
- Minimum adjustments for both Simplified Income and Simplified Taxes.
- Simplified Income: excludes dividends, equity gains/losses, and illegal payments; simplified adjustments when PPA in financial accounts (e.g. excludes goodwill); allows further optional adjustments.
- Simplified Taxes: excludes non-Covered Taxes, taxes not expected to be paid, and taxes related to excluded income; applies deferred tax accounting but simplified approach for DTLs and loss DTAs; removes requirement to allocate taxes between jurisdictions.

Key design details:
- Starting point: jurisdictional data from existing accounting systems (no need to separately report CE-by-CE).
- Integrity safeguard: each GloBE tax attribute must be allocated once and only once to a jurisdiction. MNE not eligible if there is income not allocated to any jurisdiction.
- TP reliance: MNE may rely on intragroup pricing determined in line with their TP policy (as reported on local tax returns).
- M&A simplification: no need to exclude PPA adjustments where MNE Group's financial accounts include both income and deferred taxes relating to those items, subject to conditions.
- Simplified Income: limited adjustments to CFS – removal of dividends and excluded equity gains/losses are expected to be the only adjustments in many cases.
- Integrity adjustments in certain cases: goodwill impairment, tax-free step-up in basis.
- Optional adjustments and elections available (e.g. to align jurisdictional income with local tax base).
- Items in OCI/equity: do not need to be brought into Simplified Income if related deferred taxes are at or above Minimum Rate and also reported in equity or OCI.
- Simplified Taxes: based on income tax expense in financial accounts + deferred tax accounting.
  - DTL exclusion: deferred tax expense movements related to DTLs that are not Recapture Exception Accruals are excluded (avoids need for DTL recapture tracking – "one of the key simplifications identified by BIAC").
  - DTLs for cost recovery allowances on tangible assets or capitalised R&D: included.
  - Net deferred tax expense is recast at the Minimum Rate using a simplified methodology.
  - Valuation allowances and recognition adjustments disregarded – loss DTAs appropriately recognised.
  - Simplified approach for cross-border PE income/taxes (PE Simplification Election).
- Continuity: re-entry rules prevent complexity from MNE routinely moving between safe harbour and full GloBE computations.

Next steps:
- IF to consider information reporting obligations for SESH.
- Work ongoing on de minimis and routine profits test safe harbours (scheduled for first half 2026).
- Anti-arbitrage rule to be developed to prevent shifting GloBE Income and Covered Taxes between jurisdictions.



### Section 1.2: Overview of the SESH (Box 1.2, pp. 14–23)

Safe Harbour Test
- "At the election of the Filing Constituent Entity, the Top-up Tax in a Tested Jurisdiction for a Fiscal Year shall be deemed to be zero" where:
  - (a) Tested Jurisdiction has a Simplified ETR >= Minimum Rate (15%), where Simplified ETR = Simplified Taxes / Simplified Income, or
  - (b) Tested Jurisdiction has a Simplified Loss.

Tested Jurisdiction
- Consists of Constituent Entities (including PEs), JVs, or JV Subsidiaries for which a separate ETR is required.
- Annual Election to include non-MOCE Constituent Entities and Same-country Investment Entities as a single Tested Jurisdiction.
- "Same-country Investment Entity" = Investment Entity or Insurance Investment Entity if all CE-owners also located in the same jurisdiction and none have made an election under Article 7.5 or 7.6.

Source of Information
- Simplified Income and Simplified Taxes calculated from financial accounting data used to prepare the CFS (consistent with Articles 3.1.2 and 3.1.3).
- Exception: QDMTT LFAS jurisdictions must use the local financial accounting standard for Simplified ETR Safe Harbour computations, unless the QDMTT LFAS jurisdiction permits CFS use.

Simplified Income (summary rule, detail in Section 3 below)
- Start from JPBT (Jurisdictional Profit or Loss before Income Tax).
- JPBT = aggregate Financial Accounting Net Income or Loss of CEs + Jurisdictional Income Tax Expense (JITE).
- Basic Adjustments: remove Excluded Dividends; remove Excluded Equity Gains or Losses; add back bribes/kickbacks/illegal payments; add back fines and penalties >= EUR 250,000.
- Industry Adjustments (financial services, shipping).
- Conditional Adjustments.
- Optional Adjustments.

M&A Simplification (rule, detail in Section 3.4.2 below)
- MNE Group may apply M&A Simplification if all assets (except goodwill) and liabilities for which a GloBE-to-book Difference is identified have the same tax basis before and after the M&A Transaction and have corresponding deferred taxes accrued at or above Minimum Rate.
- Result: GloBE-to-book Difference effect not removed from JPBT; accrual of related DTAs/DTLs not included in Simplified Taxes; reversal of DTAs/DTLs attributable to M&A Transaction (incl. non-REA DTLs) IS included in Simplified Taxes.
- Goodwill impairment or amortization without corresponding DTL (or with DTL below Minimum Rate): added back to JPBT; reversal of related DTL excluded from Simplified Taxes.
- Article 6.3.4 election: available in a Safe Harbour year where the tax basis of assets/liabilities is adjusted to fair value in connection with a triggering event (incl. M&A Transaction) in the current Fiscal Year.
- Also available for M&A Transactions in prior Fiscal Years where Article 6.3.4 was not available that year. In such case, GloBE-to-book Difference included in Simplified Income either in year of election or ratably over 5-year period.

Simplified Taxes (summary rule, detail in Section 4 below)
- Start from JITE = sum of current and deferred income tax expense/benefit in FANIL of CEs + deferred taxes recorded at consolidated level attributable to CEs.
- Adjustments:
  - (a) Policy-based: reduce by current/deferred tax expense for non-Covered Taxes (Article 4.2.2); adjust for refunds and credits (Article 4.1.3(b) and (c)).
  - (b) Correlation adjustment: reduce by tax expense associated with items not included in Simplified Income (Articles 4.1.3(a) and 4.4.1(a)).
  - (c) Uncertain taxes/taxes not payable promptly: adjust for uncertain tax positions (Articles 4.1.2(c), 4.1.3(d)); reduce by current tax not expected to be paid within 3 years (Article 4.1.3(e)); adjust under Article 4.4.6 for uncertain tax positions and taxes on distributions.
  - (d) Deferred Tax Adjustments:
    - Deferred tax expense RECAST at Minimum Rate formula: Deferred Tax Expense in Simplified Taxes * (Minimum Rate / Accounted Tax Rate), where Accounted Tax Rate = income tax rate used to recognise deferred taxes in the relevant financial accounts (before any valuation allowance).
    - Deferred tax expenses reflected without regard to any valuation allowances or accounting recognition adjustments.
    - Adjusted for changes in income tax rate (Article 4.4.1(d)).
    - Non-REA DTLs EXCLUDED. "The DTL Recapture Rule generally does not apply under the Safe Harbour."
  - (e) Optional Adjustments.

Simplified Adjustment for Negative Taxes (loss years)
- In a FY with both Simplified Loss and negative Simplified Taxes, must determine Simplified Adjustment for Negative Taxes (SANT).
- SANT = Simplified Taxes – (Simplified Loss * Minimum Rate), if negative.
- SANT is carried forward and included in Simplified Taxes (or Adjusted Covered Taxes in non-safe-harbour years) in the same manner as the Excess Negative Tax Carry-forward.
- Alternative (Loss DTA Adjustment during Transitional Period): available if MNE had a tax loss, Simplified Loss, and Net Negative Taxes for the Transition Year and each preceding FY in the Transitional Period.
  - Loss DTA Adjustment = (i) loss DTA in JITE (without regard to valuation allowance/recognition adjustment) attributable to permanent differences (incl. non-economic deductions and exempt income), recast at Minimum Rate, + (ii) DTLs accrued in FY attributable to goodwill and other intangibles with indefinite life, recast at Minimum Rate.
  - Transitional Period = 5 consecutive FYs starting with Transition Year.
  - Net Negative Taxes = JITE adjusted for valuation allowances and recognition adjustments is nil or negative.

Optional adjustments
- GloBE elections under Chapter 3 of the GloBE Model Rules are available. List includes: portfolio shareholding dividends (Article 3.2.1(b)); Equity Investment Inclusion Election (3.2.1(c)); FX hedging as excluded equity gain/loss (3.2.1(c)); Debt Release (3.2.1(i)); Stock-based Compensation Election (3.2.2); Realization principle election (3.2.5); capital gain spread over 5 years (3.2.6); intra-group transactions election (3.2.8).
- NMCE Simplified Calculation also available.
- Required adjustments unless 5-Year Election not to make them: (a) Asymmetric Foreign Exchange Currency Gain or Loss (Article 3.2.1(f)); (b) Accrued Pension Expense (Article 3.2.1(i)).
- Annual Election to include Covered Taxes accrued as expense but not included in income tax expense (Article 4.1.2(a)).
- Annual Election to include in Simplified Taxes any Covered Taxes related to equity-reported item of income included in Simplified Income.
- Annual Election to include QRTCs and MTTCs (accounted as tax reduction) in both Simplified Taxes and Simplified Income.
- SBTI Safe Harbour election available.
- GloBE Loss Election (Article 4.5) available.

Transition Year rules
- Transition Year = first year MNE elects SESH for the Tested Jurisdiction.
- Articles 9.1.1 to 9.1.3 apply in determining Simplified Income and Simplified Taxes.

Tax adjustments after year end
- Default: increases/decreases to Covered Tax liability/income for a FY accrued after end of that FY (transaction year) are included in Simplified Taxes/Income of the accrual year.
- 5-Year Election: include all increases/decreases in Covered Tax liability and income accruing within 12 months of end of transaction year in the transaction year itself. Does NOT apply to TP adjustments.
- Special rule: if a net decrease to Covered Tax liability for a previous FY accrued more than 12 months after the end of that FY would cause Simplified ETR to drop below Minimum Rate, MNE may exclude that net decrease from accrual year Simplified Taxes if either: (a) the Simplified ETR for that previous FY is still not below Minimum Rate after adjusting, or (b) the GloBE ETR for that previous FY is not below Minimum Rate after adjusting.

Cross-border allocation of income and taxes
- Must follow Articles 3.4, 3.5, and 4.3.2(b) for PEs and Flow-through Entities.
- Taxes allocable from Main Entity to PE or from CE-owner to subsidiary CE under Article 4.3.2(a),(c),(d),(e) are EXCLUDED from all Tested Jurisdictions, EXCEPT withholding taxes imposed on distributions.
- Exception: 5-Year Election to allocate such taxes in accordance with Articles 4.3.2 and 4.3.3. If allocated to a CE in a non-QDMTT jurisdiction, included in Simplified Taxes.
- PE Simplification Election: available for PEs of Main Entities in jurisdictions with anti-hybrid rules consistent with BEPS Action 2 AND a taxable branch regime. Condition: deferred taxes on the Main Entity's domestic income/expenses not reflected in JITE below Minimum Rate.
  - Under PE Simplification Election: PE Simplified Income included in Main Entity Tested Jurisdiction income (to the extent treated as income/loss under the taxable branch regime); Main Entity's taxes related to PE income included in Main Entity's Simplified Taxes; foreign tax credit used by Main Entity to reduce tax on PE income included in Main Entity's Simplified Taxes; Simplified Income/Taxes of PE Tested Jurisdiction determined without regard to Articles 3.4.5 and 4.3.4.
  - Annual Election, made on jurisdictional basis.
  - Where a PE Simplified Loss has been included in the Main Entity jurisdiction, MNE must continue making the election in each subsequent FY until the Main Entity has included an equal amount of Simplified Income of that PE.
  - Foreign tax credit cap = (total foreign tax credit used in year) * (PE income included in taxable income / total foreign source income included in taxable income). Cannot exceed nominal tax rate of Main Entity jurisdiction * total PE income included in Main Entity taxable income.

Transfer pricing adjustments
- Cross-border TP taxable income adjustments made after end of FY: included in accrual year JPBT. TP taxable income adjustment = difference between transfer price recorded in financial accounts at end of FY and transfer price used for taxable income.
- Related Covered Tax liability changes also included in accrual year.
- Where TP adjustment is made in one Tested Jurisdiction with no corresponding adjustment in the counterparty: an adjustment equal to the TP adjustment must also be made to the counterparty's JPBT.
- 5-Year Election: include TP taxable income adjustments and related tax changes accruing within 12 months of end of transaction year in the transaction year itself.
- At-cost transactions: seller's JPBT adjusted to reflect the transfer price used for taxable income (or ALP if not taxable). Buyer uses carrying value in financial accounts for Simplified Income (except intangible assets, which use ALP). Buyer uses corresponding DTA (recast at Minimum Rate) in Simplified Taxes.
- Intragroup transactions in same Tested Jurisdiction resulting in a loss: adjusted per Article 3.2.3.

Tax Neutral Entities
- Flow-through UPE or PE under Article 7.1.4: Simplified Income/Taxes reduced per Article 7.1. Deemed zero if all Ownership Interests held by Qualified Persons.
- UPE or CE subject to Deductible Dividend Regime: Simplified Income/Taxes reduced per Article 7.2. Deemed zero if: (a) all Ownership Interests held by Qualified Persons AND (b) all income distributed as Deductible Dividends.
- Tax Transparent Entity (non-UPE): Simplified Income/Taxes deemed zero if all income and taxes allocated to PEs under 3.5.1(a)/4.3.2(a) or to CE-owners under 3.5.1(b)/4.3.2(b).
- Investment Entity Tax Transparency Election (Article 7.5 or Article 10.2 definition): Top-up Tax of Investment Entity/Insurance Investment Entity deemed zero.

Ineligible Tested Jurisdictions
- Not eligible for SESH: (a) Stateless CEs (except if Tax Transparent Entity with all income allocated per 3.5/4.3.2(b)); (b) Investment Entities (except as provided for above); (c) Tested Jurisdictions where Eligible Distribution Tax System election under Article 7.3 has been made and there is an outstanding balance of the Deemed Distribution Tax Recapture Account at beginning of FY.

First Election and Re-entry Requirements
- First election: eligible if MNE did NOT have a Top-up Tax liability for that Tested Jurisdiction in every FY beginning within 24 months before the first day of the FY for which SESH is elected.
- Re-entry (after failing/not electing in a FY): eligible only if MNE had no Top-up Tax liability in any FY beginning within 24 months of the first day of the FY for which safe harbour was not elected, under EITHER (i) full GloBE Rules or (ii) any Specified Safe Harbour.
- "Specified Safe Harbour" = Simplified Calculation Safe Harbour for NMCEs and any other safe harbour IF agrees should be treated as such. Note: TCSH does NOT qualify as Specified Safe Harbour (because of its "once out, always out" approach). QDMTT Safe Harbour also does NOT qualify because the MNE might have had Top-up Tax under QDMTT while electing the QDMTT Safe Harbour.
- Re-entry rule does not consider Additional Current Top-up Tax liability attributable to a previous FY.

Integrity rules
- Four principles MNE must satisfy:
  - (a) Matching principle: intragroup income not recognised later than corresponding expense; amounts match.
  - (b) Full allocation principle: all income allocated to a Tested Jurisdiction.
  - (c) Single expense and loss principle: each expense/loss deducted once, in a single Tested Jurisdiction.
  - (d) Single tax principle: taxes recorded only once, in a single Tested Jurisdiction.
- Matching principle not satisfied: adjust JPBT in Tested Jurisdiction of CE that recognised the expense to eliminate that expense. Exception: if matching failure is solely because one party uses LFAS, the matching principle is treated as satisfied.
- Full allocation principle not satisfied: include income in correct Tested Jurisdiction per Model GloBE Rules.
- Single expense/loss principle not satisfied: adjust JPBT to eliminate the expense in all Tested Jurisdictions that recognised it.
- Single tax principle not satisfied: adjust JITE to eliminate the tax expense.
- Intragroup financial instruments: must be classified as debt or equity consistently for both issuer and holder, based on issuer's financial accounting standard.
- Economically deducted only once = single expense principle met, even if recorded by more than one CE (e.g. recharge arrangements).

Applicability date
- Filing Constituent Entity can elect SESH for FYs commencing on or after 31 December 2026.
- Optional provision: can elect from 31 December 2025 if: (a) QDMTT Safe Harbour applies to the Tested Jurisdiction; (b) only one jurisdiction has taxing rights; or (c) all jurisdictions with taxing rights have made SESH available from 31 December 2025 under their legislation and the MNE makes the election in all those jurisdictions.



### Section 2: Simplified Jurisdictional ETR (pp. 24–27)

- Safe harbour applied on a Tested Jurisdiction basis. For details on tested jurisdiction composition, see Boxes 1.2 and 2 (largely the same text).
- Calculated from financial accounting data used to prepare CFS, consistent with Articles 3.1.2 and 3.1.3.
- QDMTT LFAS jurisdictions: if MNE required to use LFAS for QDMTT computations, must also use LFAS for SESH in that jurisdiction, unless the QDMTT LFAS jurisdiction permits CFS.

Source of information:
- The IF "recognises that using the CFS accounting standard in both GloBE and QDMTT calculations for all safe harbour jurisdictions allows MNE Groups to centralise their compliance process and thereby reduces compliance burden."
- QDMTT LFAS jurisdictions encouraged (not required) to allow MNE to use CFS for SESH.
- Consistency requirement: if MNE elects to use CFS in one QDMTT LFAS jurisdiction, must use CFS in ALL jurisdictions that provide the election. This election is also irrevocable from one FY to another (must use same standard consistently).
- IF will publish list of Authorised Financial Accounting Standards allowed for SESH in QDMTT LFAS jurisdictions.

Jurisdictional aggregate data:
- MNE does NOT need to compute CE-by-CE; it may use jurisdictional aggregate data directly from ERP/accounting systems, provided that data is consistent with a simple aggregation of CE-level Simplified Income and Simplified Taxes.
- Where using aggregated data produces the same outcome as CE-level aggregation, MNE may rely on those systems.
- CE-level data still needed for PEs, Tax Transparent Entities, determining location under Article 10.3.4(b), etc.
- Currency translation rules from GloBE also apply to SESH.



### Section 3: Simplified Income or Loss (pp. 28–39)

3.1. JPBT
- JPBT = aggregate FANIL of CEs in the Tested Jurisdiction + JITE (excluding deferred income taxes in consolidated accounts).
- FANIL = calculated per GloBE Model Rules, using financial accounts used in preparation of CFS.
- Consolidation adjustments to eliminate intragroup transactions must be reversed so that profits/losses from intragroup transactions are recognised in JPBT (except for same-Tested-Jurisdiction transactions where Article 3.2.8 election has been made).
- PPA amounts reflected in aggregate FANIL should be reversed, except as provided under M&A Simplification (section 3.4.2).

3.2. Basic Adjustments

3.2.1. Excluded Dividends
- JPBT adjusted to remove Excluded Dividends per Article 3.2.1(b). Avoids double taxation and prevents Simplified ETR being depressed by non-taxable participation exemption income.
- Insurance companies: movement in policyholder reserves economically matching an Excluded Dividend from a security held on behalf of a policyholder is not allowed as expense (excluded from JPBT).

3.2.2. Excluded Equity Gains or Losses
- JPBT adjusted to remove Excluded Equity Gains or Losses per Article 3.2.1(c). Prevents distortion vs. GloBE ETR.
- Same insurance company policyholder reserves treatment as above.

3.2.3. Policy Disallowed Expenses
- Bribes, kickbacks, and other illegal payments excluded from JPBT per Article 3.2.1(g). Applies "without regard to any financial accounting disclosure requirements."
- Fines and penalties >= EUR 250,000 (per penalty) added back to JPBT. This EUR 250,000 threshold is higher than the full GloBE Rules threshold, to simplify compliance burden.

3.3. Industry Adjustments

3.3.1. Financial Services
- Insurance company income under Article 3.2.9 excluded from JPBT, unless MNE makes Annual Election not to apply the exclusion.
- Payments/receipts in respect of AT1 and RT1 Capital: Article 3.2.10 applies regardless of whether AT1/RT1 is treated as debt or equity in financial accounts. Symmetry: where AT1/RT1 income/expense accounted in equity/OCI is included in Simplified Income per Article 3.2.10, any corresponding tax accounted in equity/OCI is included in Simplified Taxes.

3.3.2. Shipping
- International Shipping Income and Qualified Ancillary International Shipping Income per Article 3.3 excluded from JPBT, unless MNE makes a 5-Year Election not to apply the exclusion.
- Election: made by NOT applying Article 3.3 (i.e. including shipping income in Simplified Income) and not applying Article 4.1.3(a) in Simplified Taxes.
- 5-Year Election applies per Fiscal Year for which the SESH is elected. MNE must still apply Article 3.3 in full GloBE years, and in loss years from the aggregate of International Shipping Income and Qualified Ancillary International Shipping Income.
- The 5-year period runs from the FY of the election, counting both safe harbour and non-safe-harbour years.
- Example: election in Year 1, full GloBE in Years 2 and 3 (where Article 3.3 applies), re-election in Year 4 (still within 5-year period, cannot apply Article 3.3 unless loss year).

3.4. Conditional Adjustments

3.4.1. Equity-reported Items (OCI and equity items)
- No adjustment to JPBT required for an Equity-reported Item of expense or loss (would only increase the Simplified ETR or be neutral).
- Adjustment required for an Equity-reported Item of income, EXCEPT when: (i) income subject to tax at >= Minimum Rate AND (ii) related income taxes accounted in equity or OCI. If related income tax is a DTL, must be a Recapture Exception Accrual.
- Equity-reported Items are: (a) Included Revaluation Method Gains or Losses (Article 3.2.1(d)); (b) Prior Period Errors and Changes in Accounting Principle (Article 3.2.1(h)).
- Where the waiver conditions are met: including the income and taxes (recast at Minimum Rate) would be neutral to the Simplified ETR, so exclusion causes no integrity risk.
- If income is not taxable at >= Minimum Rate: inclusion could bring Simplified ETR below Minimum Rate, so adjustment is required.

3.4.2. M&A Simplification and Article 6.3.4 election (see also detailed technical text pp. 33–37)

M&A Simplification conditions:
- All assets (except goodwill) and liabilities for which a GloBE-to-book Difference is identified for the Tested Jurisdiction must have the same tax basis before and after the M&A Transaction.
- Corresponding deferred tax assets or liabilities must be accrued at a rate >= Minimum Rate.
- If those conditions are not met, must remove GloBE-to-book Difference per the normal GloBE rules (or make Article 6.3.4 election).

M&A Simplification mechanics:
- GloBE-to-book Difference effect on JPBT is NOT removed (left in).
- Accrual of DTAs and DTLs attributable to M&A Transaction NOT included in Simplified Taxes.
- Reversal of DTAs and DTLs attributable to M&A Transaction (including non-REA DTLs) IS included in Simplified Taxes.
- Goodwill: impairment or amortization without corresponding DTL (or with DTL below Minimum Rate) is added back to JPBT. The reversal of related DTL (if any) excluded from Simplified Taxes.
- Where applicable accounting standard allows amortization of goodwill with corresponding DTLs at >= Minimum Rate: no add-back required.
- Under IFRS and US GAAP, initial recognition of goodwill DTL on business combination is generally not allowed; DTL accrual goes to goodwill rather than P&L, so no adjustment needed in practice.
- Non-REA DTL reversal from M&A: included in Simplified Taxes as exception to general rule. Does not create integrity risk because original accrual was not included in Adjusted Covered Taxes or Simplified Taxes of prior year.

Article 6.3.4 election:
- Available where tax basis of assets/liabilities adjusted to fair value in connection with a triggering event (incl. M&A Transaction) in current FY.
- ALSO available for M&A Transactions in PRIOR FY where Article 6.3.4 election was not available that year (e.g. pre-GloBE year or transitional safe harbour applied), provided tax basis was adjusted to fair value. GloBE-to-book Difference included in Simplified Income either fully in year of election or ratably over 5 years. The election applies to ALL assets and liabilities transferred in the M&A Transaction.
- If spread over 5 years and a non-safe-harbour year occurs, the pro-rata amount for that year goes into GloBE Income/Loss computation instead.

Numerical example (pp. 37–39 – Example 1: Target acquisition with no tax step-up in basis)
- Parent acquires 100% of CE1 for 1000; net equity of CE1 = 800; PPA = 200, allocated to tangible asset.
- No step-up in tax basis. DTL recognised: 200 * 15% = 30. DTL accrual increases liabilities; corresponding amount allocated to goodwill. Net equity increase = 200.
- PPA table: PPA Tangible Asset 200; DTL (15%) -30; Goodwill 30; Total 200.
- Subsequent years (Years 1–4): tangible asset depreciated ratably over 5 years = 40 of additional depreciation p.a.; DTL reversal 40 * 15% = 6 p.a.
- Effect on Simplified ETR (Years 1–4): Simplified Taxes 144 (=150 – 6), Simplified Income 960 (=1000 – 40), Simplified ETR = 15%. Neutral.
- Year 5: goodwill fully impaired (additional expense 30 on top of 40 of depreciation). Goodwill impairment added back (+30) to JPBT per M&A Simplification. Result: Simplified Income 960 (= 1000 – 40 – 30 + 30), Simplified Taxes 144, ETR = 15%.
- Without the add-back, ETR would be 144/930 = 15.5% – distortive but would not harm integrity. Yet the rule adds back to ensure accuracy.

3.5. Optional Adjustments

3.5.1. GloBE Elective Adjustments
- All GloBE elections under Chapter 3 available (listed above in Section 1.2).
- NMCE Simplified Computation available.
- If 5-Year Election made in a non-safe-harbour year, must continue to apply in safe harbour years (until revoked).
- Article 3.2.6 election (capital gain spread over 5 years): continuity issue – if look-back period includes safe harbour years, must add net gain allocable to those years to Simplified Income and re-determine Simplified ETR. If re-determined ETR >= Minimum Rate, Additional Current Top-up Tax deemed zero. If below, full GloBE computations apply for that year.

3.5.2. Optional Exclusions
- AFXGL (Article 3.2.1(f)): must be adjusted to JPBT, unless MNE makes 5-Year Election not to apply it. The 5-Year Election spans all FYs (whether safe harbour or not) until revoked.
- Accrued Pension Expense (Article 3.2.1(i)): same treatment – must adjust unless 5-Year Election not to.



### Section 4: Simplified Taxes (pp. 40–52)

4.1. JITE
- JITE = sum of income tax expense in FANIL of CEs + deferred income taxes in consolidated accounts related to that FANIL.
- Where PPA income/expense reflected in financial accounts and M&A Simplification applies: JITE includes deferred taxes attributable to PPA for the M&A Transaction.

4.2. Basic adjustments to JITE

4.2.1. Policy-based adjustments
- Remove amounts not qualifying as Covered Tax under Article 4.2.1.
- Adjust for refunds and credits per Articles 4.1.3(b) and (c).
- MNE may elect to add Covered Taxes accrued as expense but not included in income tax expense per Article 4.1.2(a).

4.2.2. Correlation adjustment (Simplified Taxes vs. Simplified Income)
- Remove tax expense for items not included in Simplified Income per Articles 4.1.3(a) and 4.4.1(a). Includes: current tax expense on dividends from non-Portfolio Shareholdings; DTAs from accrued expenses only tax-deductible when paid; Covered Tax on shipping income in cases where shipping income not included in Simplified Income.
- No POSITIVE adjustment for losses/expenses deductible for tax but not in Simplified Income.

4.2.3. Uncertain taxes and taxes not payable promptly
- Exclude: tax expense from accrual/reversal of uncertain tax positions (Articles 4.1.2(c), 4.1.3(d), 4.4.6). Such taxes only included in Simplified Taxes when actually paid.
- Exclude: current tax expense not expected to be paid within 3 years (Article 4.1.3(e)).

4.2.4. Deferred Tax Adjustments

Valuation allowance or accounting recognition adjustment:
- Ignore valuation allowances and recognition adjustments on DTAs. Ensures Simplified ETR appropriately recognises when loss carry-forwards have been offset. Same effect as Articles 4.4.1(c) and 4.4.2(c).

Adjustments for change in tax rate:
- Exclude additional or lower net deferred tax expense resulting from a tax rate change, per Article 4.4.1(d). Tax rate changes above the Minimum Rate excluded. Where Article 4.6.2 or 4.6.3 would apply (rate change crossing the Minimum Rate threshold), simplified methodology in Section 4.6 is available.

Generation and use of tax credits:
- Exclude deferred tax expense from generation/use of tax credits per Article 4.4.1(e), except for Substitute Loss Carry-forward DTAs.

Exclusion of non-REA DTLs:
- Generally: JITE adjusted to EXCLUDE deferred tax expense attributable to non-REA DTLs. Avoids need for DTL recapture tracking.
- Two exceptions:
  - (a) Reversal of DTL attributable to M&A Transaction where M&A Simplification applies (Section 3.4.2): included in Simplified Taxes.
  - (b) Reversal of DTL that was included in ETR computation under a Qualified IIR, UTPR, or QDMTT in a prior FY: included in Simplified Taxes. Rationale: DTL would have increased ETR when it accrued; if the reversal were excluded, MNE would get a double benefit.

4.2.5. Recasting of deferred tax expense (key calculation)
- Deferred taxes recast at Minimum Rate only if related DTAs/DTLs computed at a tax rate higher than the Minimum Rate.
- Simplified approach: recast NET deferred tax expense (not each DTA/DTL individually as in full GloBE Rules).
- Formula: Deferred Tax Expense in Simplified Taxes * (Minimum Rate / Accounted Tax Rate)
  - "Deferred Tax Expense in Simplified Taxes" = total deferred taxes including accrual and reversal of DTAs and DTLs taken into account in Simplified Taxes for the Tested Jurisdiction for the FY.
  - "Accounted Tax Rate" = income tax rate used to recognise deferred taxes in the relevant financial accounts (before any valuation allowance).
- For jurisdictions with single CIT rate on single tax base: Accounted Tax Rate = that CIT rate.
- For jurisdictions with multiple Covered Taxes at different rates and bases: MNE must recast using a methodology consistent with GloBE recasting principles. Further guidance to be provided by IF.

4.3. Simplified Adjustment for Negative Taxes in Simplified Loss years (see Box 1.2 above for formula)
- Formula: SANT = Simplified Taxes – (Simplified Loss * Minimum Rate), if negative.
- Loss DTA Adjustment alternative during Transitional Period (5-year period): see Box 1.2 above for details.

4.4. Optional adjustments

4.4.3. QRTCs and MTTCs accounted as tax reductions
- Default: no adjustment required; tax credits accounted as tax reduction continue to reduce Simplified Taxes.
- Annual Election: apply GloBE adjustment per Article 4.1.2(d) + Article 3.2.4 adjustment to Simplified Income. The tax credit is added to both Simplified Taxes and Simplified Income.
- Election applies to credits from current FY and prior FYs not yet fully utilised. For carry-forward credits, the unutilised amount is included in Simplified Income in year of election; election continues until fully utilised.

4.4.5. GloBE Loss Election
- Article 4.5 election available under SESH.
- Must be made in GIR for the first FY when the CE is located in the jurisdiction.
- Continues in all subsequent FYs regardless of whether safe harbour or full GloBE rules apply.

4.4.6. Other GloBE elections in Chapter 4 that are default in SESH
- These are already incorporated as default treatment, so electing them separately would be redundant: (a) Unclaimed Accrual election (Article 4.4.7); (b) Immaterial decreases in Covered Tax (Article 4.6.1); (c) Election not to allocate cross-border deferred taxes (para 71.16 in Commentary to Article 4.4.1).

4.5. Transition Year rules
- Transition Year = earlier of Transition Year per GloBE Model Rules/Commentary or first FY MNE elects SESH for that jurisdiction.
- Unlike TCSH, SESH does NOT delay the Transition Year.
- Transition Year does NOT reset when re-entering the safe harbour.
- Articles 9.1.1–9.1.3 apply to DTAs/DTLs arising before the Transition Year.
- Non-REA DTLs arising before Transition Year: reversal excluded from Simplified Taxes, UNLESS the MNE included that DTL in Adjusted Covered Taxes for a previous FY when subject to GloBE Rules or QDMTT.
- Because SESH does not extend the Transition Year, Article 9.1.3 does not apply to transfers of assets from a disposing CE in a Tested Jurisdiction that applies SESH.

4.6. Tax adjustments after year end
- Default: true-up adjustments reflected in accrual year.
- 5-Year Election: include increases/decreases accruing within 12 months of end of transaction year in the transaction year itself. Applies to all jurisdictions. Does not apply to TP adjustments. In year of election, any income/taxes accrued for preceding transaction year must be included in election year; in year of revocation, income/taxes taken into account in preceding transaction year under election excluded from revocation year.

Large refunds for prior years:
- Including refunds for previous FYs in accrual year could cause Simplified ETR to drop below Minimum Rate even when MNE would still have qualified in the prior years. In such cases, MNE may add back refunds for a specific previous FY (a "Qualified Refund Year") to Simplified Taxes in the accrual year, to the extent that those refunds would not result in a Simplified ETR below Minimum Rate in the prior FY (or GloBE ETR below Minimum Rate in a prior non-safe-harbour FY).
- Definition is "cumulative": takes into account ALL refunds accrued in respect of the prior FY, not just those in the current FY.
- Detailed numerical example (pp. 51–52): Year 1 – Simplified Taxes 300, Simplified Income 1200, ETR 25%. Year 2 – Simplified Taxes 250, Simplified Income 1500, ETR 16.7%. Year 3 – refund of 100 for Year 1 and 50 for Year 2; own income 1000, JITE 100 (250 – 150 refund). Raw ETR Year 3 = 10%. Year 1 still has ETR of 16.7% after adjusting for refund (300–100)/1200 – so Year 1 is a Qualified Refund Year; add-back 100, revised ETR Year 3 = (50+100)/1000 = 15%. Year 2 would drop to (250–50)/1500 = 13% – NOT a Qualified Refund Year. In Year 4, additional 80 refund for Year 1 would make Year 1 ETR = (300–100–80)/1200 = 10% – no longer Qualified Refund Year.



### Section 5: Cross-border Income & Taxes (pp. 53–58)

## Chapter 4 – Substance-based Tax Incentive Safe Harbour (pp. 70–78)

### Section 1: SBTI Safe Harbour (pp. 70–71)

- "The SBTI Safe Harbour eliminates the Top-up Tax that would otherwise be attributable to QTIs."
- Reduces Top-up Tax where, and to the extent, the Top-up Tax is attributable to use of QTIs.
- Election by Filing Constituent Entity: amount of Top-up Tax corresponding to QTIs for the FY deemed zero.
- Top-up Tax corresponding to QTIs = difference between: (i) Top-up Tax calculated under the QTI treatment (paragraph 4 of the box), and (ii) Top-up Tax that would have been calculated without the election.
- Mechanism: Adjusted Covered Taxes increased by the LOWER of:
  - amount of QTIs used in the FY, and
  - the Substance Cap for the Tested Jurisdiction.
- QTI = generally available Tax Incentive where the amount is calculated based on:
  - expenditures incurred (expenditure-based), or
  - amount of tangible property produced in the jurisdiction (production-based).
  - A tax incentive includes a QRTC or MTTC when Filing Constituent Entity makes Annual Election to treat it as a QTI.
  - Expenditure-based incentive NOT qualified if the value of the tax benefit exceeds the amount of expenditure incurred.
- Amount of QTI used in a FY:
  - For tax credits: reduction in Covered Tax liability in FY due to utilisation of tax credit.
  - For super deductions/enhanced allowances: amount of enhanced allowance or super deduction claimed in FY multiplied by the statutory tax rate.
  - For income exemptions/preferential rates: amount of income attributable to eligible expenditure that is exempt multiplied by the statutory tax rate, OR amount of income attributable to eligible expenditure multiplied by the difference between the statutory and preferential tax rates.
- Substance Cap:
  - Method 1 (default): 5.5% * greater of (i) sum of Eligible Payroll Costs of Eligible Employees performing activities for the MNE in the jurisdiction OR (ii) depreciation and depletion on Eligible Tangible Assets in the jurisdiction.
  - Method 2 (elective, 5-Year Election): 1% of carrying value of Eligible Tangible Assets in the jurisdiction (excluding land and other non-depreciable assets).
  - If MNE revokes Method 2 election, depreciation and depletion for assets previously included under Method 2 must be excluded when applying Method 1. Prevents double benefit.
- Applicability: Filing Constituent Entity can elect SBTI Safe Harbour for FYs commencing on or after 1 January 2026.

### Section 2: Qualified Tax Incentives (pp. 71–74)

- QTI definition applies broadly; does NOT restrict based on type of expenditure. Does not apply to non-Covered Tax incentives, incentives for excluded GloBE Income, or subsidies/grants.
- Annual Election: treat a QRTC or MTTC as a QTI, provided the credit qualifies as an expenditure- or production-based tax incentive.

2.1. Expenditure-based tax incentives
- "The amount of the incentive must be calculated directly by reference to the expenditure incurred."
- Can be in the form of credits, super deductions, enhanced allowances, or exemptions (if exempt amount is calculated by reference to expenditure).
- Tax allowances for capital expenditure causing only timing differences (e.g. immediate expensing): NOT within scope.
- Super deductions/enhanced allowances resulting in income exclusion EXCEEDING the original investment: the excess amount qualifies.

2.1.2. Exclusion if tax benefit exceeds expenditure
- Expenditure-based incentive is NOT a QTI if the value of the tax benefit exceeds the expenditure on which it was calculated (considering all incentives for the same item of expenditure together).
- "The value of a tax benefit is the maximum amount by which the tax liability can be reduced by the tax incentive."
- For a tax credit: the amount of the tax credit.
- For super deductions/enhanced allowances/exemptions: the additional deduction or excluded income multiplied by the statutory tax rate.

2.2. Production-based tax incentives
- Based on volume of production (units produced) of tangible property in the jurisdiction. Value-based production incentives excluded.
- Includes manufacturing, electricity production, processing (extraction and refining).
- Only based on units produced in the jurisdiction providing the incentive.

2.3. Calculation based on incurred expenditure or output produced
- Incentive must be calculated based on expenditure that has been incurred or output that has been produced by the time the amount of the incentive is determined.
- Expenditure is incurred when accrued in financial accounts or when a payment is made.
- Need not be incurred in same period the incentive is determined.
- Administrative pre-clearance procedures do not disqualify the incentive, as long as the final amount is determined when costs have actually been incurred.
- Incentive utilised in a later year (after expenditure/production has occurred) still eligible.

2.4. Generally available
- Must be generally available to taxpayers. Excludes incentives restricted to in-scope MNEs.
- Excludes incentives that would not arise independently of a governmental arrangement (as per paragraph 8.3 of Commentary to Article 9.1.2): agreements, rulings, decrees, grants, or similar between MNE Group and General Government.
- Eligibility decision by a government that the taxpayer meets published or statutory criteria does NOT prevent the incentive from being a QTI.

2.5. Ongoing monitoring
- IF developing guidance on when jurisdiction benefits must be treated as returns of tax reducing Adjusted Covered Taxes (Related Benefits).
- Monitoring process to assess whether QTIs are Related Benefits. "The treatment of QTI as an increase to Adjusted Covered Taxes when no such taxes are paid means there is a similar risk an incentive could be designed so that it formally meets the definition of a QTI but in practice operates to mitigate the impact of the GMT rather than functioning as a targeted incentive."

### Section 3: Treatment of QTIs (pp. 74–77)

3.1. Adjustments for QTIs
- Adjustments made at jurisdictional level, after Adjusted Covered Taxes and GloBE Income have been calculated under Chapters 3 and 4.
- QTIs treated as increase to Adjusted Covered Taxes: amount = lower of (QTIs used in FY) and (Substance Cap).
- QRTCs and MTTCs not included in GloBE Income. When a QRTC/MTTC is elected to be treated as QTI: it is excluded from GloBE Income and treated as reduction in Adjusted Covered Taxes; then QTI adjustment increases Adjusted Covered Taxes. Substance Cap applies to total adjustment. Election can be made for part of a QRTC/MTTC.
- When QRTC/MTTC elected as QTI and treated as taxable income: the tax on the part excluded from GloBE Income is excluded from Adjusted Covered Taxes per Article 4.1.3(a).
- Numerical example in document (p. 75): Net GloBE Income 11,000 (incl. 1,000 QRTC that is expenditure-based). Pre-credit tax 1,500; reduced by QRTC 1,000 and non-refundable expenditure-based credits 500. Adjusted Covered Taxes = 1,000. ETR = 9.1% (1,000/11,000). Top-up Tax due.
  - SBTI election. Substance Cap = 1,100. Non-refundable credit adjustment: Adjusted Covered Taxes increase to 1,500; revised ETR = 13.6% (1,500/11,000). Still 600 of cap remaining.
  - Elect 600 of QRTC as QTI: GloBE Income reduced by 600; remaining 400 QRTC still in GloBE Income.
  - Revised ETR = 1,500/10,400 = 14.4%.

3.2. Amount of QTI used

3.2.1. Tax credits
- Amount used = amount of tax credit that reduced Covered Tax liability in the period. Includes carry-forward credits utilised. QTI adjustment applies AFTER Article 4.4.1(e) has been applied (deferred tax on reversal of DTA still excluded).

3.2.2. Super deductions, enhanced allowances, exemptions
- Tax value: excess of deduction over expenditure * statutory tax rate (or total deduction if otherwise non-deductible) or exempt income * statutory tax rate.
- Amount "used" = the amount of super deduction/allowance/exemption claimed (i.e. included in computation of Covered Tax liability).
- If super deduction causes a taxable loss: QTI adjustment is still made in year loss arises (increasing Adjusted Covered Taxes and reducing any Excess Negative Tax Carry Forward, which benefits MNE in later FYs when loss is utilised).
- Detailed example in document (p. 77): Jurisdiction A, 15% CIT rate. Super deduction eligible as QTI. FY X1: FANIL 1,000; taxable income 1,000; additional super deduction 1,500; taxable profit = 0; tax loss 500 carried forward; DTA recognised 75; Adjusted Covered Taxes = -75.
  - Without SBTI election: TUT = 1,000 * 15% = 150; Excess Negative Tax Carry Forward = 75. FY X2: FANIL 500; tax loss utilised; DTA reversal increases ACT to 75; but reduced by ENTCF of 75; net ACT = 0; TUT = 500 * 15% = 75. Total TUT over two years = 225.
  - With SBTI election: QTI adjustment = 1,500 * 15% = 225. Adjusted Covered Taxes = -75 + 225 = 150. Revised ETR = 150/1,000 = 15%. No Top-up Tax in X1, no ENTCF. In X2: ACT = 75 (DTA reversal only). ETR = 75/500 = 15%. No Top-up Tax. Total = 0.

3.2.3. Income at preferential rate calculated based on expenditure
- Tax value = income subject to preferential rate * (statutory tax rate – preferential rate).
- Example: statutory 20%, preferential 15%, eligible income 2,000; value = 2,000 * 5% = 100.

### Section 4: Substance Cap (pp. 78)

- Method 1 based on SBIE factors (payroll / tangible asset depreciation) but uses the HIGHER of the two.
- Eligible Payroll Costs in Method 1 includes capitalised payroll costs (those excluded from SBIE under Article 5.3.3(a)). No need to exclude them because Method 1 is based on either payroll OR tangible assets (not both).
- Method 2 (carrying value) designed for incentives given on initial capital expenditure – tends to provide greater relief in early years of asset life. Alignment with timing of receipt of tax incentive benefit.
- If Method 2 revoked: assets that used carrying value must be excluded from depreciation calculation under Method 1. Prevents switching between methods to maximise benefit.



## Chapter 5 – Side-by-Side System (pp. 79–87)

### Section 1: SbS Safe Harbour (pp. 79–85)

Summary:
- Applies where MNE Group UPE is in a "Qualified SbS Regime" jurisdiction.
- Election: Top-up Tax for ALL jurisdictions for a FY deemed zero for purposes of IIR and UTPR.
- Also covers JV and JV Subsidiary interests.
- Does NOT affect QDMTTs in any jurisdiction.
- Does NOT affect MNEs whose UPE is in a non-Qualified SbS jurisdiction.

Qualified SbS Regime requirements (para. 3 of Box):
- (a) Eligible domestic tax system.
- (b) Eligible worldwide tax system.
- (c) Foreign tax credit for QDMTTs on same terms as other creditable Covered Taxes.
- (d) Eligible domestic AND worldwide tax systems enacted prior to 1 January 2026 (or later date per Central Record procedure).

Eligible domestic tax system (para. 4 of Box):
- (a) At least 20% statutory nominal CIT rate, taking into account preferential adjustments and sub-national CIT where applicable.
- (b) QDMTT OR corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) based on financial statement income, subject to appropriate adjustments consistent with minimum taxation policy objectives, at nominal rate >= 15%, applicable to "a substantial portion of the aggregate income of in-scope MNE Groups' operations in the jurisdiction."
- (c) No material risk that in-scope MNEs headquartered there will have ETR on overall domestic profits below 15%.

Eligible worldwide tax system (para. 5 of Box):
- (a) Comprehensive tax regime on all resident corporations for foreign income, broad base: includes active AND passive income of foreign branches and CFCs regardless of whether distributed; only limited exclusions consistent with policy of minimum taxation (e.g. generally high-taxed income); NOT including highly mobile income (e.g. royalties).
- (b) Substantial mechanisms to address BEPS risks (e.g. preventing foreign tax credits on active income from sheltering low-taxed passive income; requiring separate basket treatment for low-tax CFCs vs. high-tax CFCs).
- (c) No material risk that in-scope MNEs headquartered there will have ETR on overall foreign profits below 15%.

Central Record:
- IF will list qualifying jurisdictions on Central Record.
- Assessment by end of first half 2026 for requesting IF member jurisdictions.
- Later requests (2027 or 2028): assessed timely on same basis, taking into account that GMT via QDMTTs is the primary system.

Application details:
- SbS Safe Harbour available from FYs commencing on or after 1 January 2026 (or later as listed in Central Record).
- Applies to ALL CEs (including Stateless CEs and MOCEs), JVs, and JV Subsidiaries of the MNE Group.
- Top-up Tax also reduced to zero under IIR at the level of Intermediate Parent Entity.
- Does NOT benefit entities that would be JVs of an eligible MNE but whose UPE is itself an MNE Group subject to GloBE Rules.

Nominal CIT rate calculation nuances:
- Adjusted for preferential adjustments: if taxpayers entitled to a deduction equal to 25% of income from a 20% tax, effective rate = 15%. Exclusions from taxable income and tax credits calculated similarly.
- Sub-national taxes: combined rate must be >= 20% in all sub-national jurisdictions; the sub-national jurisdiction with the lowest combined rate is the relevant rate.

CAMT requirements:
- Corporations subject to the higher of CAMT liability and regular CIT. May be offset by foreign/domestic tax credits consistent with minimum taxation objectives.
- Must apply to "a substantial portion" of aggregate income of in-scope MNEs in the jurisdiction. Threshold-based CAMT (applying only to large MNEs) can satisfy this if the threshold is set such that a substantial portion of the collective net income of all in-scope MNEs is covered.

Material risk test:
- Assessed holistically, over time, at aggregate level (not per MNE Group individually).
- A "material risk" = "a risk that is of significant magnitude and probability that policymakers would foreseeably consider it as the basis for revising an applicable tax regime."
- ETR slightly below 15% for certain types of income but with substantial other income highly likely taxed above 15% → may still satisfy the criterion.
- Incentives with potential scope and size creating material risk of ETR below 15% → does NOT satisfy.
- Can be met due to operation of CAMT; may take into account anti-hybrid rules (BEPS Action 2) and interest limitation rules (BEPS Action 4).

Eligible worldwide: comprehensive regime
- Must include CFC income regardless of distribution. Only limited exclusions consistent with minimum taxation (e.g. high-tax income exclusion but NOT royalties).
- Substantial BEPS mechanisms: must prevent high-tax active income FTCs from sheltering low-tax passive income.

Information Reporting for SbS Safe Harbour:
- Election made in GIR, in a field to be added to Section 1.
- MNE electing SbS Safe Harbour provides only Section 1 of GIR to IIR/UTPR jurisdiction (not required to complete Section 1.4 high-level summary).
- Still subject to QDMTT filing: provide Section 1 (excl. 1.4) and the Jurisdictional Section to QDMTT jurisdiction.
- Aggregated GIR and QDMTT data on anonymised basis to be part of peer review and monitoring.

Notification of material changes:
- Jurisdiction must notify IF within 3 months of material amendment to Qualified SbS Regime. "Material" = could have foreseeably impacted the IF determination.
- Examples: reduction in CIT rate, repeal of CFC tax regime, new income exclusion/exemption/preferential regime.
- Not required to notify for changes to aspects NOT taken into account in the no-material-risk determination.
- Still required to notify if materially expanding availability of tax incentive or preferential regime.

### Section 2: UPE Safe Harbour (pp. 86–87)

- Applicable from FYs commencing on or after 1 January 2026. Replaces the Transitional UTPR Safe Harbour (which expired end 2025).
- Election: Top-up Tax for the UPE Jurisdiction deemed zero for purposes of UTPR.
- Qualified UPE Regime = jurisdiction has an eligible domestic tax system (same three criteria as above) enacted and in effect on 1 January 2026.
- Effect: Top-up Tax for ALL CEs in the UPE Jurisdiction deemed zero for UTPR purposes.
- Does NOT affect IIR or UTPR for CEs outside UPE Jurisdiction.
- Does NOT affect QDMTTs.
- Eligible domestic tax system criteria = same as for SbS Safe Harbour.
- Enacted before 2026: system enacted on 1 January 2026 remains eligible despite non-material subsequent changes.
- Information Reporting: GIR amended with additional election field. No additional info required to demonstrate eligibility (GIR Section 1 already identifies UPE and jurisdiction).
- Notification of material changes: same as for SbS Safe Harbour.
