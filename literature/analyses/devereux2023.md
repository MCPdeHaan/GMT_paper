# Devereux, Paraknewitz & Simmler (2023) — "Empirical Evidence on the GMT: Critical Mass and SBIE"
**Fiscal Studies, 44(1), 9–21**

**P.9**
Focus of paper: Incentive of countries to implement GMT depends on features of proposal and on if operations in countries that implement GMT. Conclusion: If a significant mass of 3 or 4 large countries implement, other countries will follow.

**P.10**
Revenue from top-up tax collected under GMT = top-up rate × excess profits (= financial profits - Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE)).

SBIE is a proportion of the value of tangible assets and payroll costs.
Key factor in revenue collected: size of SBIE relative to financial profits. SBIE also has an effect on tax competition.

Profit from real activities (instead of profit shifting) generate relatively large SBIE -> small top-up tax -> countries continue to compete to attract real activities.

GMT implemented in EU -> total taxation of 9% of financial profit in scope in short run, 12% in long-run.

**P.14: Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE): Formulaic carve-out**
Initially sum of 10% of payroll and 8% tangible assets (an average of beginning and end of financial year), percentages will decline gradually until 10th year, then 5% for both payroll and tangible assets.

SBIE is deducted from GloBE income to derive excess profit. Base for top-up tax levied, could be QDMTT, IIR, UTPR.

Higher SBIE -> reduced top-up tax -> lower ultimate tax rate levied.

Analyze size of SBIE: Unconsolidated financial statements of foreign-owned EU subsidiaries of MNEs, data from Amadeus database.
Data from 2019 on sales, pre-tax profits, wage cost, tangible assets, depreciation provisions.

Measure of interest: Share of pre-tax profit covered by SBIE -> exclude firms with non-positive profit.
Also other measure of: ROE, thus exclude if non-positive equity.
Some part of profit non-taxable (dividend received).

Do not observe complete ownership structures and no aggregated revenue for consolidated MNE -> apply analysis to all foreign-owned firms in EU27 and UK (43,564 firms) -> test robustness by seeing if firm is in scope.

**P.15**
Size of SBIE relative to pre-tax profit: Heterogeneity among firms, SBIE below pre-tax profit for around 70% of firms, for remaining 30% -> SBIE exceeds pre-tax profit, sometimes by wide margin.

Unused SBIE cannot be carried forward -> SBIE cannot exceed pre-tax profit (adjustment was needed).

Average ratio of SBIE to pre-tax profit: 57%, median 52%, fall to 41% and 28% respectively after 10Y due to reduction to 5% SBIE.

Average ratio SBIE to pre-tax profit of 57% -> excess profit is on average 43% of GloBE income -> minimum tax of 6.45% of GloBE income. Rising to 8.85% after 10 years (see Figure 1).

Share of total pre-tax profit shielded 37% in 1st year and 23% after 10Y.

**P.17**
Share of total profit covered by SBIE declines with statutory tax rate, but only to small degree.

For some countries (Luxembourg, Malta, Ireland) have lower share of profits covered by SBIE than for other EU countries.

Ownership of assets: Unused SBIE since SBIE > pre-tax profit.
Effect: Incentive for firms with unused SBIE to mix with in-scope firms with SBIE that is insufficient to prevent top-up tax by exchanging assets and associated income stream, or merging firms.

In-scope MNE acquires profitable domestic firm -> combined profit subject to GMT -> How far would that reduce top-up charge of MNE?
Size of unused SBIE of other foreign-owned firms small relative to total profit of foreign-owned firms.

**P.19**
Heterogeneity by sector and source finance: Heterogeneity in ratio of SBIE to pre-tax profit. 2 factors influencing this:

* Use of intermediate material: Some firms produce intermediate material themselves (using tangible assets and labor) and other firms buy intermediate materials.
* Produce self -> higher ratio of SBIE to pre-tax profits.
* Lowest average ratio of SBIE to profit: retail & finance -> retail needs intermediate material, finance not -> no conclusion.


* Ratio of SBIE to pre-tax profit depends on use of debt finance: Greater use of debt will tend to decrease pre-tax profit -> higher ratio of SBIE to pre-tax profit.
* Evidence: Share of profit covered by SBIE increases substantially with firm's debt ratio.
