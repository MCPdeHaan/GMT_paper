# Detailed notes

Yareba 2023: The global minimum tax raises more revenues than you think, or much less

P. 1
Aim GMT: limit profit shifting to low-tax jurisdictions.
Focus: strategic tax setting effects, modelling location decisions of firms.
ETR of jurisdiction in low-tax jurisdiction: $\frac{\text{taxes paid}}{\text{GloBE income}}$. If below 15% $\rightarrow$ group pay top-up tax to bring ETR to 15%, applied to GloBE income of subsidiary, after adjustments to tax base (substance based income exclusion, SBIE) and top-up taxes (qualified domestic top up tax, QDMTT).

P. 2: 
2 high-tax countries, 1 tax haven (ETR below GMT), not model QDMTT nor SBIE, assume tax haven collects revenue otherwise collected by non-haven (incentive when tax rate is endogenous). SBIE reduces top-up tax, but not eliminate incentive to compete for mobile capital.

Direct effect GMT: reduction in profit shifting $\rightarrow$ first order positive effect on revenues in high tax countries due to increase in tax base $\rightarrow$ higher taxes attractive at margin.
$\hookrightarrow$ Secondary effect: for non-havens the value of attracting real FDI increases due to less profit shifting $\rightarrow$ intensify competition among non-havens $\rightarrow$ push tax rates down.

If profit shifting reduced by GMT and tax rates in non-haven countries increase $\rightarrow$ offset some of revenue gain in non-havens from less profit shifting.
2 types of non-haven instruments:
(distortionary) tax rates $\rightarrow$ Section 2, government choice variable.
(lump sum) subsidies $\rightarrow$ Section 3 conditional on being located in that country, regardless of profit shifting decisions, no direct relationship to taxable corporate profits.
Long-term outcome GMT: upward adjustment of CIT.

P. 3
Tax rates $t_1, t_2$ for non-havens and $t_h$ for havens, $t_{min} = \text{gmt rate}$.
Assume $t_h < t_{min} < t_i, i=1,2$. $t_{min}$ captured by exogenous increase in $t_h$, assumption: revenue from GMT goes to tax haven.

P. 4
Do revenues in non-haven countries increase?
$s =$ gross profit, earned regardless of location.
Parent firm profit from producing/selling in country $i=1,2$: $\pi_i = (1-t_i)[s-g_i] - C(g_i)$.
$g_i =$ TP paid by subsidiary in tax haven.
$C(g) =$ convex cost function for shifting profits, true price of intermediate normalised to 0, deviations from true price are costly.
Firm shifts profits out of non-haven into tax haven. Subsidiary profits in haven:
$\pi_h^i = (1-t_h)g_i$
Optimal profit shifting price: $g_i^* = g_i(t_i, t_h)$
Maximise these formulas w.r.t. $g_i$: $\Pi_i = \pi_i + \pi_h^i$
Equalisation of marginal benefits (tax savings) and marginal concealment costs:
$C'(g_i^*) = t_i - t_h, \quad i=1,2$
Increase in haven's tax rate reduces profit shifting $\rightarrow$ raises firm's tax base in non-havens:
$\frac{\partial g_i^*}{\partial t_h} = \frac{-1}{C''(g_i^*)<0}$, representing source of revenue gains for non-haven government from GMT. Equation shows distortionary nature of tax.
Tax Base adjusts via optimal transfer pricing decision.
$F =$ additional fixed costs of operating in country 1 compared to operating in country 2, but $F$ not observable to government.

P. 5
Marginal firm has fixed costs: $\hat{F} = F(t_1, t_2, t_h, g_1^*(t_1, t_h), g_2^*(t_2, t_h))$
where $B_i^* = s - g_i^*$ is tax base, taking optimal profit shifting into account.
$F \leq \hat{F}$ operates in country 1, $F > \hat{F}$ in country 2.
Increase in haven's tax rate affects fixed cost threshold:
$\frac{\partial \hat{F}}{\partial t_h} = \frac{\partial \pi_h^1}{\partial t_h} - \frac{\partial \pi_h^2}{\partial t_h} = g_2^* - g_1^*$
Change in non-haven tax rate affects marginal firm via:
$\frac{\partial \hat{F}}{\partial t_1} = -B_1^*, \quad \frac{\partial \hat{F}}{\partial t_2} = B_2^*$
Increase in own tax rate could drive some firms out of the country.
Tax rate competition:
for non-haven countries: $R_i = m_i(\hat{F})[t_i B_i^*]$
haven countries: $R_h = t_h [m_1(\hat{F}) g_1^* + m_2(\hat{F}) g_2^*]$
Non haven countries maximise by choosing tax rates in simultaneous Nash game.
Max non haven revenue wrt $t_i$: FOC: $\frac{dR_i}{dt_i} = -m t_i B_i^{*2} + m_i(\hat{F}) (B_i^* - t_i \frac{dg_i^*}{dt_i}) = 0$
(Note: First term represents loss in tax revenue from firms leaving country due to higher tax. Second term represents effect on tax base of firm).
Proposition 1: Symmetric Nash equilibrium: non-haven tax rate does not decrease after introduction of GMT, tax revenues in non-haven countries increase $\Rightarrow$ GMT raises tax revenues more than mechanical effect.
Proposition 2: Assume that non-haven countries compete via tax rates for continuum of multinational firms, which locate real activity in one non-haven country and have quadratic concealment costs for profit shifting.
Economic impact of GMT on tax revenue, investment and profit shifting difficult to estimate due to combined effect of BEPS and Pillar Two $\rightarrow$ Behavioural effects and responses of governments difficult to estimate.

P. 10: Subsidies can be used as tool to counter GMT, countries have stronger incentive to adopt non-tax subsidies to attract investment of multinational enterprises. In practice EU limit State Aid with Art 92(1) TFEU.

P. 12: Welfare maximisation may lead to partially different outcomes relative to revenue maximisation. Findings on lump sum instruments robust. Distortionary tax competition shows that subtle effects complicate analysis further.
Larger countries more attractive locations for multinational firms, since saves transportation costs $\rightarrow$ higher tax rates are more sustainable.
Economic impact of GMT on tax revenue, investment and profit shifting difficult to estimate due to combined effect of BEPS and Pillar 2. Predicting behavioral responses by MNEs in investment and profit as well as responses of governments w.r.t. domestic CIT policies also difficult to estimate as a result.
P. 14: Tax revenue effects of GMT depend on if competition is distortionary or lump sum instruments.
Distortionary tax rates exogenous $\rightarrow$ But gov. compete for firms with lump sum subsidy $\rightarrow$ GMT leaves net tax revenue in non-haven countries unchanged, while increasing in haven country. MNEs benefit in this game. Firm specific subsidies is common in US $\rightarrow$ high chance that firms will use them.
Countries compete via distortionary tax rates $\rightarrow$ GMT raise/lower non-haven tax rates and tax revenues $\rightarrow$ important to allow tax rates to adjust endogenously.
Shifting profits easy $\rightarrow$ initial tax competition for firms is intense $\rightarrow$ revenue in non-havens rise.
Danger of offsetting incentives is real.
Incentives will become attractive.
It matters how tax base is calculated.
